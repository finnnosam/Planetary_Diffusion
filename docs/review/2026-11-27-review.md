# Independent terrain review · 27 November 2026

**The largest failure in the finished terrain is the loss of mountain belts during landscape evolution. The largest widespread failure in the plate terrain is the shortage of low ground deep inside continents.** They occur at different stages and need different fixes. The current plate model gets the amount of land and its mean height reasonably close. It can put a mountain belt in the right place for the landscape to use. On the one current, matched end-to-end example, the landscape reduces the mean elevation of the parent’s highest land from **3,337 m to 638 m**. It is worth finding out why before making stronger mountains upstream.

I would keep the remap candidate out of the defaults and finish its repair first within the plate work. Two short replays reproduce the start of the basement failure and identify a local transfer error that survives removal of the planner’s suspected plate-wide correction. On observed Earth, the landscape law still overpredicts basin denudation by a median factor of 8.4 with the slope cap disabled. I would move the erosion diagnosis ahead of the investigation into continental thermal age. That is a proposed change to the agreed order.

## Findings, ranked by terrain impact

### 1. The landscape removes most of the high terrain its forcing can support

**High confidence for seed 1001 and the existence of the failure. Its frequency across seeds is unmeasured.** I reconstructed the endpoint the fine landscape would reach with surface processes switched off. It uses the actual initial surface and noise, 30 one-Myr increments of load and surface motion along the saved parcel paths, and the production n = 868 flexure operator. Because flexure is linear, the endpoint needs one deflection of the summed load. The experiment switches off the fine-grid surface processes only. The parent’s ocean evolution and sea-level history still enter through the saved forcing.

| n = 868 surface | Land p99 | Land above 3 km | Mean height at the *same locations* above 3 km on the present plate parent |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Earth | 4,706 m | 3.064% | — |
| Plate parent interpolated onto the fine grid | 3,079 m | 1.258% | 3,337 m |
| Initial surface of those parcels, 30 Myr earlier | 3,066 m | 1.096% | 1,339 m |
| Present with plate forcing and flexure alone | 5,553 m | 4.982% | 4,448 m |
| Present with legacy erodibility | 2,734 m | 0.644% | 2,349 m |
| Present with corrected erodibility | **1,557 m** | **0.014%** | **638 m** |

The matched-location comparison matters more than the percentile. The forcing builds about 3.1 km of elevation where the parent ends high. Surface processes and their flexural response then lower those locations by 3.81 km relative to the forcing-only endpoint. Journal 142 measures the omitted continental lithospheric support at +70 m signed mean and 352 m mean absolute change on a related, persistently continental high-land mask. Carry that support for physical consistency. Its measured magnitude and mixed sign give little reason to expect it to rescue these mountains. ### 2. Continental interiors are perforated, and the remaining lowlands are too close to the sea

**High confidence in both effects. Moderate confidence that the proposed remap is the best eventual fix.** I repeated the inland-distance calculation with two definitions of water on each unmodified surface. The second uses only the largest connected body below sea level. Land and elevations are identical in the two readings.

| Share of that distant land below 200 m | 19.4% | 3.9% | 10.5% |
| Land ≥300 km from the main ocean | 68.6% | 53.8% | 58.1% |
| Share of that distant land below 200 m | 22.0% | 14.3% | 16.9% |

The nominal fivefold lowland deficit shrinks to about 1.5-fold on A when isolated depressions cease to define the coast. That makes the remap work worthwhile. It also sets a limit on the explanation. Even with isolated water removed from the distance calculation, A has too little interior and too little low ground in it. The difference between masks does not tell us which depressions should be lakes, dry depressions or shallow seas. The global water-volume solve counts all cells below the level regardless of access. Keep the two readings side by side. ### 3. Thick crust is present. Broad, very high plateaus are scarce before landscape evolution begins

**High confidence in the shortfall across sampled maps. Moderate confidence in the explanation.** A’s p99 falls between 2,781 and 3,641 m across the six endpoints. Its highest 1% of land is split into pieces even after each planet gets its own height threshold. I also searched every 10 Myr from the start to the present. Across 156 default maps, the single largest connected area above 4 km was **0.492 million km²**, versus **2.100 million km²** on present Earth. C’s maximum was **0.387 million km²**. These are maxima across six related histories per arm. They establish what this archive achieved, not a probability of forming Tibet. [History probe](../../outputs/review-astra/2026-11-27/plateau_history.py) · [All snapshots measured](../../outputs/review-astra/2026-11-27/plateau_history.csv)

On common 1° cells, A has **more** continental area with 60–65 km of total crust than unglaciated Earth, 1.06 versus 0.84 million km², yet its median elevation there is **2,855 versus 4,023 m**. In the 65–70 km bin A has only 0.36 versus 0.92 million km², and stands at 3,608 versus 4,616 m. No seed in either arm has a majority-continental 1° cell averaging 70–80 km, where Earth has 0.81 million km². In the 45–50 km bin A is already **higher**, 1,245 versus 708 m. Raising the datum or increasing general thickening would push the wrong part of the distribution up. The model lacks both the thickest coherent roots and enough elevation at intermediate plateau thickness. The common smoothing scale matters because CRUST1.0 assigns one column to a 1° cell. [Matched thickness bins](../../outputs/review-astra/2026-11-27/interiors.csv) · [CRUST1.0 input](../../data/crust1/crsthk.xyz)

The code has two relevant mechanisms. It treats pressure-dependent densification as equilibrium throughout the lower-crust fraction, explicitly an upper-bound assumption. It also removes crystalline thickness beyond 65 km on a 10 Myr timescale. Those make testable predictions about what happens during sustained shortening. The snapshots do not establish which dominates, or whether sufficiently long-lived convergence was supplied. Select the broadest sustained collision by its incoming continental volume and duration, before inspecting elevation. Compare the incoming flux, root growth, spreading, foundering and vertical support in that belt. Judge a plateau where there was an opportunity to build one. ### 4. The ocean has too much young floor and too little relief at comparable depth

**High confidence in the two mismatches. Moderate confidence about their causes.** A has 47.4 million km² of floor younger than 10 Myr against 29.9 million km² on Earth’s dated grid. That is surviving young area, so it alone cannot separate creation from consumption. The project independently measures production of 5.19 versus 3.37 km²/yr over the last 100 Myr on the model versus present-day Earth kinematics. Ridge length is already close, 96,000 versus 94,800 km. The first physical check is integrated normal opening speed times ridge length, compared with newly created material area and the surviving age cohorts. The model’s 39 m deep-floor neighbour difference versus Earth’s 77 m is a separate deficiency. The basin profile already puts the 30–60 Myr mean depth within 87 m of Earth. A further age–depth fit would change that mean without supplying the missing variation. ### 5. The meso loses mountain detail even on Earth input

**High confidence for the archived evaluation, moderate for geographic generalization.** With true coarse Earth inputs, the model supplies 0.46 and 0.49 of observed 5.8–11.5 km band power in the hills and mountains classes. With the corrected planet landscape the ratios are 0.27 and 0.34. The evaluation selects 16 Earth windows per class and has only nine planet mountain windows. The 14.6% mountain-window Earth reference comes from held-out geographic boxes, not an area-weighted census of the world. I agree with the planned redesign. I disagree with journal 146’s attribution of “about half” the planet gap to the meso. The Earth-input control demonstrates an intrinsic deficit, but the two inputs have different distributions even within class. The ratios do not partition causes. Freeze Earth test regions, relief classes and preprocessing for the replacement. ## Decisions and next work

Sea-relative land area, location and height are the appropriate user-visible outcomes. The distinction in the record matters. [Original target](../planet-layer/journal/122_2026-11-19_pass-93.md) · [22 November decisions](../planet-layer/journal/141_2026-11-22_astra-review.md) · [26 November decisions](../planet-layer/journal/149_2026-11-26_workflow-reset.md) · [Current STATE](../planet-layer/STATE.md)

1. **Repair the transfer on a small physical example first.** Rotate a normal-basement patch beside an accreted arc. Remove a known volume of forearc material within the physical inputs’ allowance. Require local debit only against available arc material, conservation of the requested and available volume, and zero change on distant basement. Track genuine subaerial erosion separately. Repeat with a newly exposed cell and an ownership change. A simple global clamp can create crust while passing the floor assertion. Rerun the short traces, then the candidate-configured suite. Keep six development seeds and the frozen confirmation rule for any terrain claim.
2. **Test river incision on Earth, then replay the belts.** Check the spatial support of the erosion law. It applies channel incision to the full area of an 11.5 km cell. Group the basin residuals by observed runoff and channel steepness, then test whether discharge or a measured relationship between channel incision and basin-wide lowering predicts rates in held-out regions. These are competing explanations for the 8.42× excess with the slope cap removed. On the saved planet forcing, compare parcel-time erodibility with the fixed map, tracking gross rock removed, deposition, export, flexural rebound and final height on the same parent highlands. Replicate on a second development seed before claiming a general terrain gain. I would reserve one causal replay ahead of the thermal-age work.
3. **Measure the physics of the lithosphere in kilometres and metres.** The planner’s “age advances 5.5 Myr per Myr” is a symptom of using an inverse thickness table as a clock. With the actual function, 4 m of thickening on a 40 km column maps a 1,800 Myr effective age to 3,600 Myr, while the table thickness changes by about **0.002 km**. Material selection also changes an area mean. Diagnose the change in thickness, mass and uplift from mixing, mechanical strain, cooling and resets on persistent parcels. This preserves the valid concern that C pushes the surface down. Mean effective age alone cannot say how much it does so. [Thickness and inverse](../../packages/planet-tectonics/src/planet_tectonics/lithosphere.py) · [Age history](../../outputs/planet-calibration/precheck-p111-confirm/lith_age.txt) · [Journal 150](../planet-layer/journal/150_2026-11-26_pass-111.md)
4. **Keep the 500 Myr drift as a separate lifetime constraint.** I checked crystalline continental volume in both long-run series. During elapsed years 250–500 it falls 16.7% on seed 1001 and 10.7% on 1002. Land ends at 22.6% and 21.5%. That matters for the requested longer-term generator. It does not explain the 30 Myr landscape collapse. Check collision, rifting and sediment-to-crust conversions by interval before extending the integration. The simplest explanation consistent with the evidence is that the plate transfer roughens material near sea level, the plate physics produces too little coherent extreme high ground, and the landscape erodes much of the high ground it receives. Each has a distinct observable signature. A single adjustment to sea level, mantle support or meso detail cannot address all three.
