**1. The reference.** HC (Becker's propagator code, SEATREE `modules/mc/hc`) was cloned into the cloud workspace; its
no-GMT build was refused by the session's permission classifier ("code from external"), as in pass 45. It was not
worked around. Instead an independent reference was written that shares no code and no convention with
`planet_tectonics.mantle_flow` (no propagator, no A matrix, no state vector):
- **Derivation** (sympy, `precheck-p48-cloud/derive.py`, printout `derive.out`): u_r = U(r) P_l, u_θ = V(r) dP_l/dθ,
  p = Π(r) P_l, η(r), body force −g δρ P_l r̂, the spherical stress divergence, Legendre's equation to remove
  P''. Each projection comes out free of θ, as it must:
  continuity r U' + 2U − L V = 0; r-momentum 2η U'' + (2η' + 4η/r) U' − (L + 4) η U/r² − L η V'/r + 3 L η V/r² − Π'
  − g δρ = 0; θ-momentum η V'' + (η' + 2η/r) V' − (2Lη/r² + η'/r) V + η U'/r + (η'/r + 4η/r²) U − Π/r = 0;
  σ_rr = −Π + 2η U', σ_rθ = η (r V' + U − V)/r.
- **Solution** (`colloc.py`): Chebyshev collocation, one domain per constant-viscosity layer, the layer holding the
  load split at the load; U, V, σ_rr, σ_rθ continuous between layers; σ_rr jumps by g σ at the load (the body force
  integrated across the sheet); free slip at the surface and at the CMB. Read out as D defines its kernels: KH =
  −σ_rr(a)/g and KV = u_h(a) per 1 kg/m².
- The raw collocation matrix is badly scaled (layers 5 km thick next to loads, viscosity ratios to ~6,000): at
  N ≥ 28 it drifted off by up to 0.33 for loads within 5–15 km of a layer boundary (only the reference jumped between
  neighbouring depths; at N = 12 it already sat on D's value). With row / column equilibration (Ruiz, 30 sweeps) it
  converges at N = 12 … 36 to the fifth digit.

**4. What Earth does with slab mass below 660 km (the two readings).**
- (a) Earth's lower-mantle slabs carry about half the mass anomaly flux × residence × α(z) implies. Damping and
  finite resolution lower tomographic amplitudes by about this much (journal 67), which would make the proxy, not the
  record, low. No physical sink of that size is known: conduction conserves the anomaly at degrees 1–31 (√κt ≈ 80 km
  over 200 Myr); the depleted harzburgite layer's compositional buoyancy offsets of order 15% (a rough estimate:
  ~25 km at −1% against +2% for 7 km of basalt, against ~3·10⁶ kg/m² of thermal excess after α); stagnation above 660
  delays mass, it does not remove it (the proxy holds more than flux × time above 660 km, 0.28 against ~0.13).
- (b) The response couples deep loads to the surface too strongly. `sh08` (lower mantle 10²³–10²⁴ Pa s) was built to
  fit the geoid; flow models that fit the geoid over-predict the observed long-wavelength residual topography. That
  is the field's known disagreement (Molnar et al. 2015; Hoggard et al. 2016). The observation's scale itself is not
  in doubt here: Yang & Gurnis (2016) invert geoid, gravity and residual topography together and get ~1 km
  long-wavelength amplitude, Valentine & Davies' 0.74 km peak (the 100–500 m estimates rest on a constant admittance).
- Neither is the solver and neither is the planet's: both apply to Earth too. They can be split only like for like:
  **Earth's own subduction history through D's record and kernels against Valentine & Davies' spectrum** (E3). If E3
  over-predicts ~2×, the response (b) is the defect and a viscosity profile tested on Earth (geoid and residual
  topography together, the rule fixed before) replaces `sh08`; if E3 matches, the tomography is low (a) and the
  planet's excess is its flux alone. E3 needs a plate model with topologies through ≥ 200 Myr (e.g. Müller et al.
  2019, 0–250 Ma, EarthByte / Zenodo) and pygplates to extract trench locations and consumption per Myr. The
  EarthByte data on the device are Seton et al. 2020 age grids and 2019 rotations and isochrons, no topologies. That
  is a download only the user can make, so it goes to the user (it does not block B).
- Not to do: change the viscosity profile now (picking a profile that brings the planet into band is band-fitting),
  scale the flow, or change the band.
