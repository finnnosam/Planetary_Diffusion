Its five ranked findings:

1. **Too much shoreline, too little low ground behind it** (high confidence in the failure; its cause open). The match
   holds when the emerged area is matched by shifting the shoreline (45.4% four-ring land, 3.86% low flat interior). It
   also holds when distance is measured from the largest water body only (57.9% against 73.9%). It holds on 1° support,
   and on all six seeds. In the model, 97% of the area of the one-cell water holes is continental and surrounded by
   continent. Their median contrast with their neighbours: −261 m elevation, **+0.4 m dynamic topography**, −168 m bulk
   sediment. The broad field can move a rough surface across sea level. It does not make the holes. These elevation
   holes are distinct from the plate-ownership pinholes in the tests.
2. **The ocean is too smooth and renews too fast** (high confidence in both; moderate in a common cause). The mean deep
   floor (4,203 against 4,205 m) checks basin capacity, not floor geography. The model's intra-oceanic convergence,
   34.8k km, is all trench. Earth's 38.2k splits 20.8k trench and 17.4k other convergence. So instant initiation can be
   tested. The age-depth law fitted to the conditional mean supplies no within-age relief.
3. **Late drift in the 500 Myr run** (seed 1001, `outputs/planet-plates/long-500-seed1001-20261122`). From elapsed 250
   to 500 Myr: crystalline continent 7.08 → 5.90 billion km³, land 28.2 → 22.6%, mean land height 889 → 1,342 m, plates
   40 → 64. Net crystalline loss 1.28 → 4.74 km³/yr between the halves. Booked crystalline erosion 3.00 → 8.03 km³/yr.
   Second-half collision consumes 22.2 points of the globe in area; rifting restores 10.7 and back-arc 3.4. The
   ledgers close; the slab record closes. The six 250 Myr runs also lose crystalline crust faster at the end (1.52
   km³/yr over the last 40 Myr against 0.57 over the run). Past 250 Myr, `split_rule` 'earth' holds Earth's youngest
   rate (no cycle of its own). The long 1001 is not the short 1001's start: the mantle draws knots for the requested
   horizon first.
4. **The landscape hand-off gives active belts old-rock erodibility** (high confidence in the error; its effect on the
   finished terrain unmeasured). `sim.py` writes `orogeny_age_myr` = t − last orogeny, which is 0 in an active belt.
   `coupling.PlateHistory.sample` turns NaN into 0 before interpolating. `coupling.build_forcing` then sets every age
   ≤ 0 to 10,000 Myr (`since = np.where(orog > 0, orog, 1e4)`). So `coupling.erodibility` gives 0.15 where the young
   belt law gives 1.0. In mixed stencils, missing and finite values blend. On one n = 868 location per coarse quad with
   all four corners finite and exactly 0: 17.0% of sampled land, and **96.6% of land above 3 km** (93.6–99.2% by seed).
   The landscape uses this map through its whole 30 Myr window.
5. **Broad high plateaus are missing although the tower vetoes pass** (moderate priority). 85% of Earth's > 4 km land is
   in the Himalaya–Tibet box. Test whether the model can sustain a plateau given a suitable collision history. Don't
   require a Tibet per seed.
