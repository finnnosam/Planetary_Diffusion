#### 3. Where the spread is

- **It is not the high land.** The continents' upper half is Earth's: p50 / p75 / p90 349 / 796 / 1,332 m against 328
  / 744 / 1,378 (by crystalline crust ≥ 30 km 397 / 841 / 1,384 against 367 / 812 / 1,438). The land's p90 is Earth's
  (1,427 against 1,414). The low tail is not: p5 / p10 / p25 −591 / −325 / +45 m against −33 / +28 / 144, 22% under
  the datum against Earth's 7% on that set. Land p50 stands high because the land is the upper part of a continent
  whose low part is drowned.
- **Not the dynamic topography.** The interiors' excess against Airy is large and the planet's field lifts them: on
  land the field averages +157 m and the interiors (600–1,200 km) +302 m, against Earth's +9 and −97 m (Holdt et al.
  2022, degree 1–30; `dyn_inland`). Earth's continental crust sits on its lows (−69 m; −118 / −184 / −653 m on
  40–45 / 45–55 / > 55 km), the planet's on +11 m (`dyn_crust`). But taking the field out (z − dyn, without
  re-solving) leaves the planet's continents 20% under the datum (from 22%) and their p10 at −200 m (`dyn_tail`):
  the field only changes which crust is drowned (40–45 km 0.14 → 0.03, 30–35 km 0.47 → 0.58). D, the plate-aware
  field, stays parked (its amplitude, journal 74).
- **The drowned continent is not Earth's kind** (`drowned_band`, label-free continent by elevation about its sea). On
  Earth the continent at −1,000 … −200 m is 2.0% of the surface: thin rifted margin, crystalline crust 24 km, 82%
  within 300 km of the edge, 2.5 km of sediment. On the planet ('burial') it is 7.9%: crust median 31 km (51% of it
  30–40 km), 407 km inland (42% within 300 km of the edge), 2.0 km of sediment, the field −261 m, lithosphere ~1.1 Gyr.
- **It comes in with the start.** At −250 Myr 5.2% of the surface is continent at −1,000 … −200 m: crust 37 km,
  ~3,000 km inside the supercontinent, lithosphere ~1.9 Gyr, the field −257 m, and 296 m of cover (high ground 831).
  It has 2 km of sediment by 0 Myr and is still drowned.
- **The start's cover is placed blind to the surface** (`cover_accommodation`). With b the bare level (the surface
  on the datum less the cover's own isostatic lift, the planet's densities), on Earth's stable domain (CRUST1.0 total
  ≥ 20 km, Precambrian / platform, > 500 km from Bird's boundaries, ice out) the cover's partial rank correlation with
  b given the crystalline crust is **−0.76** (r(S, b) −0.79, r(S, Tc) −0.33): Earth's stable cover lies where its
  column is low, and the drowned stable crust carries 2,059 m against 1,430 above. On the planet's start it is
  **+0.20** (r(S, b) −0.09), cover under the datum 623 m, above 1,594: the copula of pass 89 (crust and a random
  field) knows nothing of the start's lows, so the supercontinent's basins begin starved, and below wave base the
  shelf law carries little into them (κ·e^(−depth/50 m)). By 0 Myr the planet reaches −0.37 / −0.42 (Earth on its
  whole continent −0.66).

So the spread's low side is the cover's place again, at the start: the start's sediment is Earth's in amount and
distribution but not in where it lies. This is the sediment column (b), which still ranks, read by place instead of
crust bin.

#### 5. Why this and not the others

A start that places Earth's cover on Earth's terms is a construction fix with two measured correlations and no fitted
number; it does not lift the continents by a constant, does not touch the dynamics and does not change the start's
crust, volume or cover distribution. The alternatives read this pass are weaker: the plate-aware field (D) is parked
and its amplitude is 2–4× the observation; the field's placement would lower the continents further (Earth's
continents sit on its lows); marine transport below wave base (`shelf_kappa_m2_yr`, a band-picked value whose
measured replacement the audit names) acts on the dynamic part of the starvation, and stays the next candidate if the
start's effect fades (§7).
