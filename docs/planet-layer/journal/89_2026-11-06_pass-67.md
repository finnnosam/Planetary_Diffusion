- Young margins become passive at Earth's pace up to 50 Myr; from 50 Myr on half the planet's dated margin has a
  boundary within 300 km where Earth's has one at a fifth to a quarter; and the 100–200 Myr margin is 0.48× Earth's
  length. The ridge sits at about half Earth's distance at every margin age (implied half-rates 1.9 / 1.5 / 1.1 / 0.8
  cm/yr against 2.9 / 2.7 / 1.8 / 1.3): the young oceans open half as fast. In the 'lengths' force law a fresh rifted
  continental plate has no slab: its target speed is (1.5 + ridge term) × (1 − 0.3 cf) ≈ 1.4 cm/yr.
- Of the ≥ 50 Myr margin not passive (26.8k km per seed), the nearest boundary is a trench at 0.56, a transform 0.27,
  a ridge 0.09, a collision 0.07 (`near_type.py`).
- One rule puts a trench on a margin directly: `_initiate_subduction` breaks the whole ocean component holding a
  plate's > 170 Myr floor from the rest of the plate, so on a plate with continent it cuts along the coast; the oldest
  floor of a margin plate is at the margin. Earth does not do this (no unambiguous Cenozoic passive margin turned
  convergent: Stern 2004, Stern & Gerya 2018; old margins too strong to fail unforced: Mueller & Phillips 1991). But on
  combo-04 only 1 of 15 initiations split a plate with continent (control 12 of 20), read from the snapshot before
  each event: the whole start and the coast ring leave margin plates little floor older than 170 Myr. So the
  trenches at old margins are mostly trenches arriving (oceans closing, normal active margins over old floor), and
  the deficit is births. By the rules the initiation rule is built anyway and swept (it is Earth's record and costs
  nothing), and the finding becomes the record that the sweep carries: what happens to young rifted margin before it
  is passive.
