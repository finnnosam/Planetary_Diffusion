### Seventy-eighth pass (8 Nov): the first Collision Lithosphere Sweep stopped: **on the thicken arm the lithosphere ratcheted without bound** (a design error of pass 77). Fixed by two mechanisms Earth has, convective removal of over-thick lithosphere (bound 237.6 km) and delamination where a root founders; the sweep scorer no longer stops on a broken run. A full-length pre-check (seed 1007) is bounded but shows thickening at collisions does not make Earth's lithosphere–crust relation. Finding for §0's next step: **C carries ~55% of Earth's measured lithospheric elevation contrast** (Artemieva's L(t) against the seismic L that Lamb's Δρ was derived with). Sweep re-armed (rule unchanged)

**1. What stopped** (`sweep-20261108-190402`, run by the user). After 11 of 12 runs `sweep_plates.score` raised in
`plate_texture.texture` (no 368 km window of land). On the thicken arm, final surfaces: seeds 1002, 1003, 1004, 1006
had L up to 10¹⁰–10³⁴ km, elevations and the sea non-finite; 1001 and 1005 L up to 10⁵–10⁶ km. Seed 1001 by time:
L > 300 km on 0 / 0 / 129 / 1,153 nodes at −200 / −150 / −100 / −40 Myr, their crust p50 51–55 km. The reset arm ran
normally (land p50 959–1,453 m by seed, L max 174–232 km). No veto read; no reviews ran.

**Cause** (pass 77's `thickened_therm`): L × (T + gain) / T applied to every step's collision volume, while the
collapse flow and foundering hold the crust near 50–65 km. The lithosphere took the cumulative shortening strain, the
crust did not, and nothing removed the lithosphere. Pass 77's tests ran 40 steps at n = 48 (no foundering, little
collision); its pre-check ran 4 steps. The full-length pre-check that would have shown it was not run: it is now
(below).

**2. Fix** (two removal mechanisms; both only under 'thicken').
- **Convective removal** (Houseman, McKenzie & Molnar 1981: lithosphere thickened past the stable boundary layer
  drips away): L held at `lith_max_km` 237.6 km, C's own law at the oldest crust in Artemieva's data (3.6 Ga); a node
  already thicker keeps its L. `lithosphere.thickened_therm`.
- **Delamination**: where `eclogite_rule` founders a root this step, the node's lithosphere resets (asthenosphere
  replaces the root; Krystopowicz & Currie 2013, the rule's own source). `sim.py`.
- `plate_texture.texture` returns NaN statistics when a surface has no land window (score ∞), so a broken arm is read
  as broken and the sweep goes on.
- Tests: `test_collision_lithosphere` + the bound (400 steps of gain on a 55 km column stop at 237.6 km; an older node
  keeps its L), and the 40-step run's L ≤ bound + aging with finite elevations. Cloud: the planet-layer subset passes
  (167, 10 CRUST1.0 skips); default path identical over 70 steps (1,120 fields, 0 differ).

**3. Pre-check** (cloud, seed 1007, 250 Myr, read only, not pooled; `precheck-p77-lithosphere/seed1007_precheck.txt`).
Bounded: L max over the run 243 km (bound + aging). Land p50 1,095 → 1,021 m, p90 2,534 → 2,191 m, crust > 45 km
0.154 → 0.132, highest node 6,658 → 6,454 m. But **dL/dT 0.18 → 0.28 km per km** (Earth 6.7–7.4): L p50 by crust bin

**4. Finding: where Earth's lithosphere–crust relation lives, and what C carries of it.** Lamb's thin-crust
regions (Europe, Zealandia, West Antarctica: 26–31 km) sit on 80–92 km of seismic lithosphere, the interiors (39–46 km)
on 170–192 km: ~95 km apart, × 10.75 m per km (Lamb's k) ≈ **1,020 m** of elevation. In C's frame (Artemieva's L(t) on
tectonothermal age, Δρ 30), Phanerozoic extended crust (~300 Ma, L ≈ 106 km) and a Proterozoic interior (~1.8 Ga,
L ≈ 166 km) are ~61 km apart, × 9.09 ≈ **555 m**. C carries ~55% of Earth's measured contrast, and its pieces
come from two frames: Lamb's Δρ was derived with Priestley & McKenzie's seismic L, while C pairs it with a thermal-age L.
The shortfall (~470 m) is the size of C1's (planet −326 m without dyn against Earth's −524) and of the column's excess
at 35–45 km. This is a measurement-to-measurement inconsistency, not a fit: the next candidate is C in Lamb's frame
(L against tectonothermal age measured from a seismic lithosphere model on Earth's ages; Lamb's k), which needs a
global seismic lithosphere grid (Priestley & McKenzie 2013/2016, or Hoggard et al. 2022; not in `data/`; Artemieva's
own TC1 1° map is published too and would check C's present frame on Earth). The start's L–T join (journal 101 §5)
stays behind it: the planet's thin-crust L (99–105 km) is already near Earth's 80–92; its interiors are where the
contrast is short.
