**3. Why still no small fragments.** A 'weak' line still joins two seeds, the second among the 10% of the plate
farthest from the first: the line can bend into weak lithosphere but must divide the plate roughly in half (new-plate
area p50 10.4 Mkm² on seed 1007, the halves of 20–40 Mkm² plates). It cuts across the whole continent each time. Earth's
fragments (Madagascar, Rockall, Seychelles, Lord Howe, Zealandia, Flemish Cap, ...) are strips or blocks rifted off a
continent's rim: the rift did not run across the continent from ocean to ocean through the middle; it ran a short way
near the margin. A seed rule that "halves" is an assumption (audit), not a mechanism.

**4. The arm: `rift_line` 'chord'.** Mechanism: a rift nucleates at a point and propagates both ways until it reaches
a free edge (propagating rifts: Courtillot 1982, Tectonics 1, 239; Van Wijk & Blackman 2005, EPSL 229, 247). Plate
driving forces act at the plate's edges (slab pull, ridge push), so a plate's interior transmits a force per unit width
that rises where the plate narrows; the cut through the nucleus whose integrated strength is least fails first. Most
microcontinents are rifted off margins, many beside subduction (van den Broek & Gaina 2020, Tectonics 39). Rule:
- Nucleus: a random continental node of the plate with all its neighbours in the plate (uniform; assumed).
- Cost of an edge: as 'weak' (length × wiggle / strength; strength C's L(t) / Earth's continental mean × the belt factor).
- Cut: Dijkstra from the nucleus; the plate's edge nodes binned by direction from the nucleus (5°, a discretisation);
  over pairs of bins more than 90° apart (two branches leaving opposite ways; assumed geometry), the cheapest sum of the
  two cheapest paths **that leaves at least `piece_min_area_mkm2` (1 Mkm²) on both sides**: the model's smallest plate.
  Diagonal steps are filled to axis steps so the cut closes against an 8-neighbour fill. The new plate is the
  second-largest piece the cut leaves; the rest stays.
- If no pair qualifies (no interior continental node, an annular plate), the 'weak' bisector. Ocean-only splits keep the
  bisector (`_rift_line(..., chord=False)`).
- Same rate law (K): Earth's margin per Mkm² of continent, per km of the candidate's margin, so shorter cuts split more
  often and the margin made per Mkm² stays Earth's. Same opening (3.5 cm/yr). No new number.

Rejected while building: the cheapest cut through the nucleus without the plate floor. On the n = 48 test plate 9 of 10
such cuts hugged the plate's edge (a cap of 0–3 nodes: crumbs) and fell back to the bisector; physically they are rims
too small to be plates here. With the floor and every pair in cost order: 1–113 tries per draw, every draw a chord.
