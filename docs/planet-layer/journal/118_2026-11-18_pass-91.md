### Ninety-first pass (18 Nov): where the thin crust is missing. Diagnosis only (the one allowed in a row; pass 90 adopted). The planet's 20–30 km continental crust matches Earth's within 100 km of the continent's edge. About half of the shortfall is 200–800 km inland, and Earth's thin crust there lies near subduction zones (back-arc extension). The candidate for pass 92 is fixed: **back-arc extension**, the missing negative branch of `margin_shortening`'s rate (`max(0, u − rollback)` clips it to zero). Nothing built, default path unchanged

**1. Why diagnose first.** §0 item 2 named the thin crust (K4) and the shelf, but pass 90 named no mechanism. The obvious lever, the rifted margin's taper `rift_width_km`, was measured and kept at 100 km on 4 Nov (Chenin et al. 2017's necking domain, 10–100 km). So before building anything, this pass measured where the thin crust is short. Probes are in `outputs/planet-calibration/precheck-p91-thin/` (`thin_where.py <tag> <arms[@tag]>`, `thin_setting.py`), with printouts beside them.

**2. Where it is short** (`thin_where.txt`). Thin = total crust + sediment 20–30 km, label-free (as `crust_review`). Distance is measured from the edge of the ≥ 20 km continent. Planet: `start_sediment-all` of `sweep-20261117-195758`, 0 Myr, six-seed mean (thin share 0.098).

The edge itself is Earth's. Of the shortfall (0.057 of the continent), about 1/6 is at 0–100 km, about 1/4 at 100–200 km, and about half beyond 200 km. Earth's crust thickens gradually inland over ~800 km, from 27 to 42 km. The planet's reaches 38 km by 200 km and stays flat. Its deep interior is *thinner* than Earth's (38.8 against 41.8). Earth's thin crust 300–800 km inland lies at about sea level (mean z +14 to +20 m, ~75% under shallow seas). That is the same interior lowland the step-back found missing (journal 117: 5.9% against 19.2% of land beyond 300 km below 200 m).

**3. What it is not.**
- Not the crust's flow. The Thick Tail Sweep's 'fitted' and 'rey' arms give the same profile (thin share 0.088 / 0.084; beyond 200 km 0.018–0.027 both).
- Not the start's sediment. 'none' 0.093, 'all' 0.098.
- Not the start alone. At −250 Myr the supercontinent's interior (80% of its area) has thin share 0.029 at 38.9 km. On Earth most of today's inland thin crust was made after 250 Ma: the North Sea and Barents (Jurassic), West Siberia (Triassic), Sunda, the East China Sea, the Aegean, Pannonia and the Basin and Range (Cenozoic). So it has to be made during the run. Between −200 and 0 Myr the planet's thin share beyond 200 km stays at 0.002–0.03.

**4. Earth's inland thin crust lies near subduction** (`thin_setting.txt`, Bird 2003 PB2002 steps). Of Earth's thin crust more than 200 km from the edge (27% of all its thin crust), 57% lies within 1,000 km of a subduction step and 20% within 600 km. For all continent in the same band those shares are 19% and 7%: a threefold enrichment. That is the back-arc setting: Sunda, the East China Sea and Okinawa trough, the Aegean, Pannonia, the Tyrrhenian, and the Basin and Range behind the Farallon slab. The thin crust within 200 km of the edge is not enriched (39% within 600 km against 38% of its band).

**5. The model cannot make it.** `PlateSim._shortening_rate` sets the upper plate's deformation rate to `clip(u − rollback, 0, None)`: the upper plate's trenchward speed minus a fixed rollback of 17 mm/yr (Oncken et al. 2006). This is the kinematic identity that Heuret & Lallemand 2005 measured across Earth's subduction zones (upper-plate deformation = upper-plate velocity − trench velocity): compression where it is positive, back-arc extension where it is negative. The clip keeps the compressional half and discards the extensional half. That is a frozen sign, which is on the list of artificial constraints to avoid. No other process extends continental crust away from a rift, since the extension rules E–E4 acted on thick crust only and were removed on 29 Oct.

**6. The candidate, fixed for pass 92 (built and swept there, not re-aimed): back-arc extension.** Where u − rollback < 0 on a continental overriding plate, the plate extends at e = rollback − u, by pure shear over a back-arc band behind the arc, conserving volume. Open design questions, for pass 92 to settle from mechanism and measurement before building:
- (a) Where the stretched volume goes in a rigid raster that cannot add area inside a plate. Two options. The rifts' own way: the thinned zone feeds a gap, i.e. a back-arc split of the overriding plate parallel to the trench, which can go on to open a back-arc basin as the Japan Sea did. Or the arc and forearc block advancing trenchward over the consumed ocean. Read how the overlap resolution and `rift_pure_shear` book area first.
- (b) The trench velocity. A fixed rollback of 17 mm/yr makes every overriding plate slower than that extend. Earth's distribution of trench velocities and of upper-plate strain (Heuret & Lallemand 2005; Lallemand et al. 2008: the share of segments in extension, neutral and compression) is the measurement, and the rule should be checked against it before the sweep.
- (c) The back-arc band's distance and width, from Earth's back-arc basins and extended provinces measured from their trench.
- It accumulates state (strain every step), so it gets a full-length cloud pre-check on seed 1019.
