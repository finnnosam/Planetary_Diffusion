**6. Earth's observed spectrum, the second amplitude reference (owed since pass 45).** Valentine & Davies 2020 (G³,
`10.1029/2020GC009240`), from Hoggard et al.'s oceanic spot and shiptrack residual topography:
- Power at l = 2: most probable 0.54 km² (likely 0.47–0.70); ~0.13–0.18 at l = 10, 0.04–0.06 at l = 20, ~0.02 at l = 30;
  degrees 1–3 hold ~86% of the power to l = 30. Peak amplitudes 0.74 ± 0.10 km at l 1–3. Water-loaded (global water
  load). Davies et al. 2019 (Nat. Geosci.) give l = 2 ≈ 0.5 km² (0.25–0.85), the same estimate.
- **Normalization.** p_l = Σ_m (∫ h Y_lm dΩ)² with Dahlen & Tromp's (1998, B6) real harmonics, which are orthonormal
  (∫ Y² dΩ = 1). So the mean square is Σ p_l / 4π. Check: with the 4π-normalized reading (mean square Σ p_l), the l = 2
  part alone would have 0.73 km rms, more than the 0.74 km *peak* amplitude, which is impossible for a degree 1–3 field;
  with the orthonormal reading the rms is ~0.3 km and peak/rms ~2.5, as for a long-wavelength field.
- **Earth's rms.** Total power to l = 30 from p₂ and the 86% share, with p₁ + p₃ between 0 and 2p₂ (p₂ is the peak):
  0.55–2.4 km², most likely ~1.3 (p₁ + p₃ ≈ p₂). Water-loaded rms √(P/4π) = 0.21–0.44 km, most likely 0.32. Air-loaded
  (the planet's P-d reads the air-loaded flow), × (ρ_m − ρ_w)/ρ_m = 2,270/3,300 = 0.69: **0.15–0.30 km, most likely
  0.22 km.**
- This is everything Earth's surface carries beyond isostasy and plate cooling: slabs, plumes and LLSVPs, the upper
  mantle above 300 km and lithospheric thickness (Davies et al. 2019 put > 80% of l = 2 in flow, > 50% of l ≥ 15 in the
  lithosphere). So it is an **upper bound** on the slab-driven part.
- **The band stands.** The proxy (222 m, slabs only) is at the observed total, and P-d's band (110–460 m) lies around
  both. Journal 67's "the proxy band may sit low for a record built from a subduction history" is not supported: the
  observation, independent of tomography, says Earth's slab signal is ≤ ~0.22 km. **The planet's slab flow (727 m) is
  3.3× Earth's whole observed signal (2.4–4.7× over the range).**
- The shape: the planet's P₃₀/P₂ (1.1·10⁻³) is far below the observation's (~0.04), as is the proxy's (6·10⁻⁴): the
  observed short wavelengths come from the lithosphere and the upper mantle, which the cut at 300 km leaves to the
  planet's isostasy. P-a was fixed on the proxy and is not changed.
- Today's random field (applied on the surface, not water-loaded) is 442 m rms with 0.12 of its variance in l 1–3:
  1.4× (water-loaded reference 0.32 km) to 2× (air-loaded 0.22) Earth's whole observed signal, with the wrong shape. The
  default path is no closer to Earth here than D; it is just plate-blind. Added to the scorecard.

**7. Where the 3.3× sits (a diagnosis, not a tuning).**
- **The slab flux, 1.6×** (journal 67 section 6: 3.2·10¹³ kg/yr against Earth's ~2·10¹³). That is B's subduction excess.
- **The record by depth.** Above 660 km the planet holds what Earth's proxy holds (0.20–0.27 against 0.28·10²¹ kg);
  below 660 km it holds 1.0–1.6·10²¹ per bin against the proxy's 0.19–0.22, 5–7×. Of that, 1.6× is the flux and ~1.5×
  the proxy's known deficit (journal 67), leaving ~2–3× in the lower mantle.
- **The kernels with Earth's own mantle.** E1 sends Earth's whole tomography below 300 km (fast and slow parts) through
  the same kernels: 417 m air-loaded. That is ~1.9× the observed 0.22 km, which also contains everything above 300 km.
  So these kernels, with `sh08` and SMEAN2's density scaling, over-predict Earth's own surface by about 2× at the
  longest wavelengths. Instantaneous flow models over-predicting long-wavelength residual topography is the known
  disagreement in the field (Molnar et al. 2015; Hoggard et al. 2016); Davies et al. 2019's own model, with shallow
  structure, is only ~1.4× in power at l = 2 (0.75 against 0.54 km², ~1.2× in amplitude). Our ~1.9× in amplitude is
  larger than theirs.
- 1.6 × 1.9 ≈ 3.0 against 3.3: the flux and a kernel-side factor together nearly close it. The kernel-side factor is
  either the response itself (viscosity profile, free slip, no self-gravitation, the propagator) or the tomography's
  density scaling. The planet's slab density comes from GDH1 and α(z), not from tomography, so only the first applies
  to the planet. **That is the next check:** D's response kernels per degree and depth against an independent reference
  (HC's kernels for `sh08`, owed since pass 45, or published kernels for a stated viscosity). If they match, the
  remaining factor is in the planet's lower-mantle record; if they are ~2× high, that is a defect to fix by mechanism.
- Not to do: scale the flow, drop the band, or shorten the record to fit (all band-fitting).
