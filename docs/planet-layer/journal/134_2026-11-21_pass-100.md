#### 3. Reading

The prediction carried from journal 74 (placement lowers the continents' level, since Earth's continents sit on its
lows) did not come true: the planet's continents sit on the placed field's highs. The field is the slab record's:
lows over young slabs, highs where no slab is. On the planet the slabs ring the continents' edges (T3, continental-
side trench, 37.5k km against Earth's 20–35k) and the interiors lie far from any slab, so the interiors get highs
(C1 farther from Earth's) and the drowned normal crust, near subduction, keeps lows (−287 m). Earth's interiors sit
over its old slabs (the Tethyan and Farallon slabs under Eurasia and the Americas), lows inland. Two readings remain
open and the next pass separates them:

1. **The degree content.** The rescale lifts the flow's weak high degrees to Earth's power (its P30/P2 ~10⁻³ against
   Earth's 0.035, journal 132 §6). A deep slab's high-degree signal is the shallowest load: the slab at 300–660 km,
   a few hundred km behind the trench, under the overriding plate's margin. So the margin lows and the interior highs
   may be mostly l ≳ 10–15, where Earth's signal is not the deep slabs' (Davies et al. 2019).
2. **The slab record's geography.** The continents may not ride over their own old slabs as Earth's did (plate
   speeds relative to the slab record; the planet's continents ringed by trenches), whatever the degree.

Not a reason to undo the adoption: the arm is the more realistic model (the field is now the planet's own), it passes
every veto, and the target moved toward Earth's on both tails and on the land fraction.
