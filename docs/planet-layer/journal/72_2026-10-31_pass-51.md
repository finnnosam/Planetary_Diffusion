**6. What this changes in the reading.**
- Journal 71's "Earth's present-day slab buoyancy is about half of what D's record holds" is withdrawn. The three
  witnesses still agree with each other (with M1 the geoid's slab band asks for 0.41× and the topography for 0.34×; the
  tomography holds 1.6–3.4× less by bin). But the mass the slabs of Müller et al.'s history should carry, by
  mineral physics, is 1.25× more than D held. Earth's surface and geoid see ~0.35–0.4× of it.
- With M1 the `sh08` geoid fit drops below 0.5 (0.46 / 0.44). That is the amplitude: the VR falls as the mass grows,
  and the geoid's preferred scale is 0.41. The pattern fit hardly moves (correlation l 2–8 0.711 → 0.707, l 4–9
  0.686 → 0.669).
- What is left, all outside the slab's own buoyancy:
  1. **The observation where it is measured.** E3's largest lows sit over subduction zones, back-arcs and continents,
     where oceanic residual-depth points are sparse. Valentine & Davies' 0.15–0.30 km is a global spectrum built to be
     robust to sparsity. A like-for-like check samples E3 at the measurement points and compares it with the
     measured values there. It needs Hoggard et al.'s (2017) spot measurements (Cambridge repository; or Holdt et al.
     2022, Mendeley Data, CC BY 4.0): the user's download.
  2. **Physics left out of the radial flow:** stiff slabs that carry their weight to the plates and the 660, and weak
     plate boundaries (lateral viscosity variations; e.g. Moresi & Gurnis 1996, Billen et al. 2003, who found such
     structure lowers the surface deflection over slabs). This is a 3-D variable-viscosity solver, a large build. It
     comes only after (1) says the observation is not the cause.
  3. Kinematics of the record: the sinking rate (15 mm/yr gives −19%; outside 12 ± 3 it would be fitting) and Müller
     et al.'s own subduction flux. Not levers by themselves.
  4. Earth's other loads (dense thermochemical piles at the base would cancel part of the slab-only l 2 field). The
     planet's D has no such loads, so this would not make D's planet amplitude right.
- Decision (rule (c)): (1) first. It is cheap, decides the band's applicability before anything is built, and either
  answer is clear: if E3 at the points is ~2.5× the measured values there, the observation is not the cause and (2) is
  next; if it agrees, the global-rms band misreads a model whose signal lies where the data are sparse.
- The planet's D under 'thermo' was not run. By the same factor it would be ~1.25× its pass-47 727 m. D stays off.
