≥ 1.5 → the observation is not the cause, lateral viscosity next; (b) otherwise → report G / P and choose the next step
from the reported bracket and pattern. Reported, not scored: r against 500 random rotations of the points; the

**5. Reading.**
- P near 1 does not clear D. The circles' spread is mostly short-wavelength signal (swells, l > 9) that a slab-only
  l ≤ 31 field cannot carry, so a slab-only field at Earth's size would have P well below 1 (Holdt's own l ≤ 30 fit
  gives std 0.50 at the circles; E3 against it P 1.59). Where E3 has its power (l 1–3), it exceeds everything measured
  at the points by itself, and the measured field follows it at 0.28. Both say E3's long-wavelength field is ~2.5–3.5×
  Earth's where Earth is measured, the same as the global ratio (2.9×) and Holdt's own l 1–3 (2.6×).
- So, read with the rule's bracket and pattern: **the sparse sampling is not the cause of D's amplitude.** Pass 51's
  decision applies: "if E3 at the points is ~2.5× the measured values there, the observation is not the cause and (2)
  is next".
- The bracket (k between 0.38 and 4.0) is too wide to read alone: r is 0.31 and the null's 95th percentile is 0.27, so
  E3's pattern is only marginally seen at the points. The l 1–3 part carries all of it (r +0.36); the l 4–31 part is
  not seen (r −0.12). The flow's intermediate wavelengths over and around slabs are where lateral viscosity (stiff
  slabs, weak plate boundaries) is known to change the surface deflection most (Moresi & Gurnis 1996; Billen et al.
  2003), so that part enters the next build's test set.
- What this does not settle: Earth's other loads (basal thermochemical piles) also reduce the slab-only l 2 on Earth,
  and the planet has none. A lateral-viscosity build is judged on Earth with E3 first; if it cannot bring E3's l 1–3 to
  the data without breaking the geoid, the piles are the remaining candidate, and that would be a question of what the
  planet's mantle should contain (a direction question for the user).
