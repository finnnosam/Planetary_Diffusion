**1. What C is** (journal 77 section 6, as built).
- State: plate raster `therm` (time of the last thermal event, Myr; NaN on oceanic crust), carried with the plates like
  `orog`: world composition, new nodes, holes, split, merge, weld. Snapshots write `lithosphere_age_myr` (t − therm on
  continental crust, NaN elsewhere) in every run; it moves `elevation_m` only with `lithosphere` 'thermal_age'.
- L = 93.6 + 0.04 t km (Artemieva 2006, TC1: the regression over continental geotherms from > 3.6 Ga to the present).
  Δz = −(Δρ/ρ_m)(L − L_ref), Δρ 30 kg/m³ (Lamb et al. 2020: 20–40 net of depletion), locally compensated, × ρ_m/(ρ_m −
  ρ_w) where the surface (with the dynamic topography) is under the sea. L_ref 130.67 km: Earth's continental area mean
  of L from the same age map (journal 77's choice), so on Earth's own age distribution the term moves no volume. (The
  calibration bin's own mean, 35–40 km of crust, would be 143.7 km; the area mean keeps the continents' mean height and
  so the sea level unchanged on Earth's distribution, which is what "keep the datum" needs.)
- Class ages (`CLASS_AGE_MA`): the midpoint of the geological time-scale interval each CRUST1.0 type's name gives:
  early Archean 3.2 Ga, late Archean 2.65, Archean (Antarctica) 3.05, early/mid Proterozoic 1.75, late Proterozoic
  0.77, platforms 1.52 (Precambrian basement below the Archean), Phanerozoic 0.27, margins / shelves / transitions 0.10
  (rifted since Pangea's breakup), active orogens, arcs, rifts and extended crust 0.033 (Cenozoic). No value is fitted;
  the midpoints are an assumption (A in the audit).
- Resets to age 0: collision contacts (crust consumed under continental crust, or continental nodes on a collision
  boundary closing faster than `boundary_min_mm_yr`; not the belt clock), the continental arc belt over a converging
  trench (arc magmatism; Earth's coastal +407 m includes the Andes and Japan), new continental crust from arcs, new rift
  crust. Sediment basins that turn continental keep the floor's age. Stretching: L → L·T_after/T_before (McKenzie 1978
  uniform stretching), the age being the one that L corresponds to (≥ 0).
- Not built: coupling the mantle lithosphere to the crust's thickness (L − T rather than L): about 1% of the Airy
  slope, inside ρ_m's own uncertainty.

- Thickness rank carries little of Earth's age structure: thick crust on Earth is as often a young orogen as a shield,
  so the start's interiors drew Earth-average ages (interior 294 Myr at 0 Myr).
- Distance rank maps a supercontinent's rim onto all of Earth's young bins: half of Earth's continent lies within 636
  km of its edge, a far smaller share of the supercontinent; the start's 600–1200 km band drew ~100 Ma.
- Final: absolute distance from the continent's edge (graph distance to non-continental crust, Earth's bins 0, 50, 100,
  150, 200, 300, 400, 500, 600, 800, 1,000 km and beyond, the last pooled). Accreted belts have a width, not a share of a
  continent. Within a bin, each start craton takes one age quantile by its own craton-age rank (no random draw). Earth:
  the term averages +271 m within 50 km of the edge, 0 at 500–600 km and −135 m beyond 1,000 km.

The final run's ages are what the design wanted (coast 21 Myr, interior 1,833 Myr; 35% of the continent reset in
250 Myr), but C1 moves only from +247 to +112. Decomposed by band (`decompose.out`, band means): the C term falls 279 m
(coast +148, 600–1200 km −131; coast bands mix old and young columns), and the planet's **dynamic topography rises
+146 m inland in the C run and +238 m in the control** (coast −48 / −1): interior land sits on the random field's
highs. The field is the fitted one (`dyn_std_m` 500, power to degree 30, slope 1): its 700–2,000 km scales are the
coast-to-interior scale, and land 600 km from the sea is likelier where the field lifts it. Earth's field has 86% of
its power at degrees 1–3 (Valentine & Davies 2020) and an rms ~0.22 km (air-loaded), and cannot make such a contrast.
Without it, C1 reads +39 (control) → −233 m (C): C moves the fall by −272 m on this seed, about a third of Earth's.
Light (porous) sediment stands ~100 m above the Airy line everywhere (it does not enter the fall).
