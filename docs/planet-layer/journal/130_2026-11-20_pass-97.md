#### 2. What the drowned continent is now

`drowned_band` (0 Myr, accommodation): the continent at −1,000 … −200 m about its sea is 6.6% of the surface, crust
median 30 km (51% under 30), 299 km inland, **2,466 m of sediment** (Earth's band 2,500 m), lithosphere ~0.7 Gyr
(median). `drowned_setting.py`: 31% of it lies within 600 km of a subduction boundary (land 20%), so back-arc
stretching is not most of it; its crust is 28.6 km (land 38.5), 61% under 32 km. `sediment_where`: the drowned
continent's cover averages 3,026 m against Earth's 2,611; `cover_accommodation` at 0 Myr: under the datum 3,038 m,
above 1,478 (Earth's whole continent 3,050 / 1,316).

So the drowned continent is **not starved**. The candidate STATE §0 named for this point (transport into drowned
continent below wave base, if the start's effect fades) assumed that it was. Its premise is wrong as stated; the
question is what shapes the drowned continent once it has its cover.

Earth's submerged continent is a shelf: its shallow quarter stays above −90 m to 300 km from land, and its median
breaks below −300 m only past 300 km. The planet's deepens steadily from the coast: a ramp, the shape diffusion
makes. Earth's submerged continent is 8.4% of the surface (ice out); the planet's 12.9%.

The plate layer's marine transport has three parts: the gravity paths (`marine_sediment_kept`: deltas and the graded
shelf to 150 km, the margin wedge, then `fill_and_spill`'s turbidites, all deposit-only), the shelf law
(`shelf_kappa_m2_yr` 3·10⁴ × e^(−depth/50 m), Kaufman et al. 1991's form) and the landscape kernel's **marine
diffusion** (`Kd_marine` 2·10³ m²/yr on every sea node shallower than 1,000 m, the same at every depth). `Kd_marine`
was tuned in the 11.5 km test bed (audit) and has never been read at the plate layer (no decision row, journal 03
and 26 only). On the 46 km grid it moves ~2·10³ × 450 / 46·10³ ≈ 20 m²/yr per metre of edge across a 450 m step
between neighbours, ~430 m/Myr spread over a node: far more than the supply. Below wave base it has no counterpart
in the stratigraphic models whose form the shelf law follows (their transport decays with depth because wave energy
does), and where the cover is gone it cuts the drowned continent's crust (`_erode` books cuts below the sediment on
continental nodes as crust erosion).

#### 4. Why this and not the others

- Transport into drowned continent below wave base as §0 worded it: built in substance (it is the same item, the
  marine transport below wave base), with its direction set by what the drowned continent is. More transport into it
  would add to a cover that is already Earth's; taking off the term that ramps it is the construction fix.
- The high side (land p50 522 m): next in §0's order; the level term (+36 m) is new this pass and the drowned band is
  still 3.3× Earth's, so the low side stays first.
- Rift and back-arc thinning as the drowned crust's source: the drowned crust is thinned (28.6 km) and young in its
  lithosphere, but only 31% lies near subduction; rifted production rose to 1.57× this pass (seed spread 1,129–2,958).
  Read again on the next control with `drowned_setting`; it is the next candidate if the marine arm leaves the band.
