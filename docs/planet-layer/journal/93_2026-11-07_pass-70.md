By the rule as fixed, opening wins (ridge excess 17.2k + stalled 3.3k = 20.5k against the oblique excess 11.5k). Two
things the rule did not anticipate, both read here:
- **The transform excess is the instrument.** Bird classes oblique subduction SUB and oblique collision OCB/CCB; the
  planet's rule calls a boundary transform when its normal rate is under half its shear. On the same rule Earth has
  42.1k km of transform-near edge, the planet 47.3k (1.12×). By cause the non-passive excess (33.7k) is ridge-near
  +18.7k, transform +5.2k, convergent +9.8k. The K line's geometry is not the next arm.
- **The ridge-near edge is not slow opening.** Its ridges open at 19 mm/yr (p50), faster than Earth's near-edge ridges
  (14). Journal 89's "young oceans 0.6× as wide" read the ridge distance of margins dated by the oldest floor within
  300 km, which dates active margins by their downgoing floor (STATE watch-point): Earth's 10–25 Myr margins are
  largely trenches over young floor (Cascadia, Mexico), not young oceans. That reading is withdrawn as an opening
  measure.

**3. Where the ridge-near edge comes from** (`ridge_near.py`, `ridge_near2.py`, `rift_pairs.py`,
`earth_ridge_near.py`; outputs beside them):
- Of the planet's 41.6k km, **31.8k (24.2–38.0k by seed) has no ocean floor of its own plate in its 300 km region**:
  the ridge lies in continental crust (the ridge node flagged continental on ~24k, its column ~10–13 km). Unbroken
  rifts. The rest (9.8k) faces young own floor: ocean-ridge-near.
- Earth, by Bird's class: ridge-near via **CRB 14.8k** (d < 100 km 7.4k; opening p50 10 mm/yr), via **OSR 9.6k**
  (1.7k; 18 mm/yr). The planet's ocean-ridge-near edge is Earth's; its unbroken-rift edge is **2.1×** Earth's.
- By plate pair (event list): most unbroken-rift edge lies on pairs split 8–16 Myr ago, opening 11–25 mm/yr, the
  axis still continental at ~10–13 km (seed 1006's pair 19/36: 14,100 km of edge, split 9 Myr ago, 25 mm/yr; seed
  1003's 21/24: 11,500 km, 16 Myr, 11 mm/yr). A share is not a rift pair (2.8–13.6k by seed) or older (seed 1004:
  pairs split 55–114 Myr ago with the ridge at the continent's edge): those are a later item.
- Why the rifts stay unbroken: in pure shear the gap gets the strain-weighted mean of the zone (half-width
  `rift_width_km` 100); with the plates carrying stretched crust out of the zone at v/2, the gap crust falls by
  ~0.63 per generation, so 38 → 10 km takes ~3 generations, ~3 W ≈ 280 km of opening. Earth's pre-breakup extension
  is of that order (South Atlantic: 2.1 Mkm² of rifted margin, Brune et al. 2016, ~175 km per side), so the zone is
  not the outlier. The time is: at ~19 mm/yr the planet's rifts carry an edge for ~11 Myr.
- **Earth's rifts** (Brune et al. 2016, Nature 536, 201; plate reconstructions of Earth's major rifted margins):
  an initial slow phase (< 10 mm/yr full rate, tens of Myr; Iberia–Newfoundland 1–2 mm/yr, Gulf of California
  7.7–8.3) then an abrupt acceleration (South Atlantic: > 35 mm/yr within 6 Myr), breakup ~10 Myr after it; a third of
  the margin area forms in the slow phase, two thirds in the fast one; the cause a rift-intrinsic strength–velocity
  feedback. The planet's split opens at `split_speed_cm_yr` 2 (assumed) and relaxes to the force law: neither phase.
  K times its splits by Earth's floor production beside continent (floor ≤ 10 Myr old), which is the fast phase's
  product, so the fast-phase rate is the consistent value for the split's opening.
