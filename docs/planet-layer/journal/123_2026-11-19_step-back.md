### 19 Nov 2026 — How the generated continents compare with Earth after pass 93

The six worlds have about Earth's continental crust area and thickness. Too much of that crust lies underwater, and the land that emerges has little room for broad inland plains. All six seeds have less exposed land than Earth's reference. All six also have too few lowlands even when the comparison is restricted to land far inland. These are separate properties of the map. A future change could improve either one while leaving the other poor.

The baseline is the adopted Back Arc Sweep arm C (back-arc extension on, fixed rollback 9 mm/yr), seeds 1001–1006. Values below are six-seed means at the present-day endpoint on the 46 km plate grid unless a time window is given. The Earth values are the project's matched-grid references. “Datum” means the common reference elevation. The model's own sea averages 17 m below it. #### Why the map lacks inland plains

| Distance from the sea | Generator | Earth |
|---|---:|---:|
| Share of all land within 150 km | 47.8% | 22.8% |
| Share of all land beyond 600 km | 12.9% | 36.5% |
| Share below 200 m **among land beyond 300 km** | 6.9% (2.1–12.7% across seeds) | 19.2% |
| Median land elevation at 600–1,200 km | 698 m | 449 m |
| Median crust thickness at 600–1,200 km | 40.3 km | 41.0 km |

The first two rows sum the published distance bands. They measure how much interior exists. The third measures what occupies the interior that survives. Even at nearly equal median crust thickness, land 600–1,200 km inland stands about 250 m higher than Earth's. The exposed-land median hides this pattern because submerged low continental ground drops out of that statistic. The project's decomposition assigns +93 m of the median difference to the spread of elevations, −62 m to continental level, and approximately zero to the sea. [Coast-distance profiles](../../../outputs/planet-calibration/sweep-20261119-182758/reviews/coast_profile.txt) · [Height decomposition](../../../outputs/planet-calibration/sweep-20261119-182758/reviews/three_factor_C.txt)

Pass 93 improved the distribution of crust thickness and the shape of individual margins. At the same time exposed land fell from 26.5% to 22.5%, and the share of model-flagged continental crust underwater rose from 34% to 47% (Earth about 29%). Extension still occupies 39% of continental overriding trench compared with Earth's 11%. Both the elevation supported by a given crustal column and the placement of thin crust warrant investigation. Their respective contributions to lost land remain open. #### Implications for the next passes

1. **Keep the pass-94 diagnosis first.** Compare continental elevation on the datum at matched crust thickness using the same statistics for Earth and the generator. The preliminary comparison mixes model medians with Earth means. Use the split to rank the three candidates for pass 95: how lithospheric thickness varies with crust thickness, sediment density and compaction, and the geographic placement of dynamic topography. For sediment, compare both area and thickness. Submerged continents hold 272 versus 144 million km³ of sediment, but they also occupy more area. Their mean sediment thickness is 3,154 versus 2,611 m. [Lithosphere by crust thickness](../../../outputs/planet-calibration/sweep-20261119-182758/reviews/lith_crust.txt) · [Sediment by location](../../../outputs/planet-calibration/sweep-20261119-182758/reviews/sediment_where.txt)
2. **Read land fraction, interior extent, and inland lowland share together.** If land fraction improves, check how much of the new land extends beyond 600 km from the sea and how much of the land beyond 300 km lies below 200 m. If elevation by crust thickness improves while inland plains stay scarce, the plan already identifies upper-plate motion and the inland placement of thin crust as leads. These are comparisons for interpreting the sweep, alongside the existing adoption rule. [Coast profiles](../../../outputs/planet-calibration/sweep-20261119-182758/reviews/coast_profile.txt) · [Current plan](../STATE.md)
3. **Keep floor turnover and passive-margin survival behind the continental work.** A timing recommendation for discussion is to revisit the whole chain after the next material improvement to continental geography. The last such assessment used one older seed on 31 Oct–1 Nov. Only 6% of its 368 km windows were flat, versus 31% in the Earth validation set, and drainage paths were short. The user found the local diffusion detail believable. A fresh whole-chain reading would show whether better plate-scale geography reaches the landscape and final terrain as broader plains, longer drainage systems, and credible mountain fronts.
