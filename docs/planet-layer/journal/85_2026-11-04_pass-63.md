**1. Earth's measured margin widths** (journal 84 section 4 asked for a compilation of sections).
Chenin et al. (2017, Geosphere 13, 559; Table 2) measure the rift domains of magma-poor margins on ~20 dip seismic
sections of single rift events (Iberia, Newfoundland, Campos, Espírito Santo, Angola, Namibia, Norway, Porcupine,
Rockall, India, Pelotas, South China Sea, Aden):
- necking domain (crust ~30–35 → ~10 km, from the necking point to the coupling point): 10–100 km, median ~50,
  mean 55, sd 25 (one double neck at 208 km left out);
- hyper-extended domain (crust ~10 → 0 km): 20–70 km, median ~60, mean 50, sd 15;
- exhumed mantle: 20–110 km, median ~70;
- distal margin in total (necking point to breakup): 130–240 km, median 165;
- no width correlates with the total width or with the margin's maturity. The proximal domain (crust 30–35 km, weak
  thinning) is not measured there; Chenin & Manatschal (EGU 2025) give 35 ± 5 km for the crust of rifted margins.
- Single margins seen this pass: Somalia necking ~50 km, South Gabon > 110 km, South Namibia taper > 100 km (Sapin et
  al. 2021, Sci. Rep. 11, 8199); Guiana 200–300 km on divergent segments including the hyper-extended domain, < 100 km
  on oblique ones (Loparev et al. 2021).

What the model's W is: the half-width of the strain taper around an opening gap (`rift_pure_shear`), i.e. the zone in
which the crust thins from its full thickness to the gap's. The gap crust carries on thinning while the rift is
open and breaks up at `breakup_thick_m` 10 km, which is Chenin's coupling point; Earth's hyper-extended domain and
exhumed mantle (< 10 km) are the model's ocean floor. So W is the necking domain (plus whatever proximal thinning it
stands for), not the distal margin and not "landward limit of thinning to the COB": that total is an outcome of W, the
gap crust and the run (journal 84's W3 reads it), so taking it as W's value would set an input from the outcome's
target. Journal 84 section 4 is corrected here.
The measured necking width (~50 km) is half the model's 100 km. As a single linear taper, 100 km thins about as much
crust per km of margin as Earth's two domains together (necking ~50 km at a mean deficit of ~17 km, plus a proximal
domain of weak thinning), but the proximal domain's width and stretching are not sourced this pass, so that equivalence
is a reading, not a measurement. W at 50 km would sit inside one 46 km cell (its taper reaches only the first
neighbours), which the old node rule showed gives one-cell margins (45–70 km of thin crust per km against Earth's 140).
Decision (Claude's, by the rules: reversible, inside item 1): `rift_width_km` stays 100 km, labelled in the audit as
the necking zone plus proximal thinning at this resolution, with Chenin's numbers; no wider W is built.

**3. The passive edge: as old as Earth's, but Earth's Pangea-breakup margins are missing** (`margin_age`).
Margin age = the oldest ocean floor within 300 km on the ocean side of a passive-edge node (label-free edge, > 300 km
from any boundary; Earth: Müller et al. 2019 age grid at 0 Ma). Earth's length-weighted mean is 108 Myr, which is
Bradley's (2008) 104 Myr for the 105,000 km of passive margin he maps, so the instrument reads Earth correctly.

- The margins survive as long as Earth's: the deficit is not that margins turn active too soon. Today's young margins
  (< 50 Myr) are 0.84× Earth's; the missing length is margins 50–200 Myr old, Earth's Pangea fragmentation
  (Central and South Atlantic, Indian and Southern Oceans).
- Active edge is 0.82× Earth's (108 against 133k km) and the continent 0.84× (164 against 196 Mkm²): passive edge per
  Mkm² of continent 552 against 884 km.
- Rifts in the run: 4–14 'rift' events per run (control; mean 10), spread evenly in time (the split rate is Poisson in
  plate area, `split_rate_per_myr` 0.006, assumed). Active continental rifts on the freeze run: ridge on continental
  crust 10.2k km on average by the flag (peaks 23k at −160 Myr), 4.6k km label-free (`rift_length`). Earth: more than
  50,000 km of simultaneously active continental rifts during Pangea's fragmentation (Jurassic–Early Cretaceous),
  20,000 km in the Late Cretaceous, up to 30,000 km from the Eocene (Brune et al. 2017; their census, from Şengör &
  Natal'in 2001, includes failed rifts, so it is an upper reference).
- Continent beside ocean floor ≤ 10 Myr old, every 10 Myr (`young_edge`): Earth 22.3k km on average over 240 Myr
  (0.087 of its edge; 35–43k at 150–120 Ma), the freeze run 3.9k (0.020). Earth's figure includes active margins
  over young plates (Cocos, Juan de Fuca, back-arcs), so it is a bound, not a test; it points the same way.
- What is not yet known: whether the run makes Earth's 50–200 Myr margins and loses them, or never makes them. The
  edge history of the freeze run (journal 84) holds passive edge at 75–90k km from −220 to −50 Myr; a per-Myr run is
  needed to split production from destruction.
