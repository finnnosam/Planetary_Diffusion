Reading. **Journal 143's arm makes the surface rougher, not smoother**: +52 holes, +3.4 crossings, the low flat interior
5.12 → 2.93% of land, far low land 8.9 → 6.6%. The rescaling is not the cause (without it the same). Its `returned`

Journal 143's R4 read the unread share (≥ 5%) and the missed increments (rms ≥ 50% of the applied) as the cause; both

#### 4. The composition-map probe (rule written and committed before its runs: `rule-compose.md`, md5 6c6323dc…)

Question: does the non-bijective nearest-node read (two world nodes reading one plate node) make the per-step excess?
Arm `compose_map` 'matched' (built first with one ring and two passes; widened to two rings and four passes before the
trace that is reported, because M1 below asks for a one-to-one map).

- **M1 (the build) fails.** Matched's `returned` exceeds its `transferred` by +56 holes against default's +87; the rule
  asked for less than half (+43). The cause is structural, measured on the warm state after one rotation: the nearest
  read leaves 22,218 plate nodes shared and 7.6% of live owned nodes unread; the two-ring matching leaves 10,391 shared
  and 3.6% unread while some reads already reach 176 km. The cube's cells differ in size across a face, so a rotated
  copy of the grid is denser than the world grid in some places and sparser in others: shared reads and unread nodes
  lie in different places, and no local matching at the same resolution can pair them.
- **M2 not read by the rule.** Recorded: halving the shared reads moved the end of a step by +8 holes, +0.3 crossings,
  −0.3 points of low flat interior, inside the noise.

#### 5. What this leaves, and the next question (pass 108)

The composition's jump each step (+162 holes, +10 crossings, −2.2 points of low flat interior) is not made by the
return transfer and not by which plate node a world node reads. What stays: (a) the nearest-node resampling itself
moves node-scale structure (a cut channel, a filled pit) by a node wherever the rotated grid is denser or sparser than
the world's, whatever the map; only a plate raster finer than the world grid everywhere (or erosion on the plate's own
raster) would stop it; (b) the floor that the smoothing in pass 106 left untouched: with smooth crust and sediment the
first surface already had 313 holes, 309 islands, 45.9 crossings. One reading in the existing traces points at a large
part of it: `end_local` (the same material with local Airy in place of flexure) has 249 holes, 115 islands and 36.2
crossings against `end`'s 416 / 380 / 53.7 on every rule above. Flexure with Te 30 km supports short loads, so the
node-scale roughness of the crust and the sediment (601 m and 203 m mean deviation from their neighbours on continental
interiors) stands in the surface at nearly full height instead of compensated. Where that roughness comes from (the
resampling of (a), crust flow's fixed boundary, erosion's own channels) is the second trace's question.
