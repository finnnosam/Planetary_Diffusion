1. **The landscape removes most of the high terrain its forcing can support** (high confidence on seed 1001; how
   often it happens across seeds is not measured). On the n = 868 chain the review reconstructed the endpoint with
   surface processes off: plate forcing plus flexure alone. Locations that stand above 3 km on the plate parent
   average 3,337 m there. They average 4,448 m with forcing alone, 2,349 m with the legacy erodibility, and **638 m
   with the corrected erodibility**. Fine-grid land p99: 3,079 m on the parent, 5,553 forcing only, 1,557 finished,
   against Earth's 4,706. On observed Earth the archived one-step landscape law overpredicts basin denudation by a
   median factor of 11.4 (2,046 OCTOPUS basins; 10.7 for ≥ 2,000 km²; 7.7 for ≥ 10,000 km²). On the 677 larger
   basins, removing the slope cap still leaves 8.42×. Removing stream-power incision while keeping the cap leaves
   1.99×. The landscape also applies the *present* erodibility map through the whole 30 Myr: at parcels ending above
   3 km the mean multiplier is 1.000 under that rule and 0.828 when read along the paths. The lithosphere-support
   channel the landscape omits (journal 142: +70 m signed, 352 m absolute) is too small and too mixed in sign to
   rescue the belts.
2. **Continental interiors are perforated, and the remaining lowlands are too close to the sea** (high confidence in
   both). Measuring distance from the main water body only (§7) shrinks the fivefold shortage of low land among
   far land to about 1.5-fold on A. Even so, A has too little interior and too little low ground in it. C improves
   the interiors at a cost on the shoreline: the shallow 0–200 m water falls 6.56 → 3.92% of the wet area (below
   Earth's 6.84) on all six paired seeds, and the shore step rises 311 → 366 m (Earth 132). **The first below-floor
   crossings of C's oceanic basement are local** (10 Myr traces on seeds 1001 and 1002, matching the archived
   snapshots). Forearc erosion cuts the excess above normal basement on a world cell that combines several plate
   cells. The return then spreads the cut over all the contributors, including cells with no removable excess. A
   floor enforced on the averaged input does not enforce it on each recipient. The plate-wide correction, journal
   150's suspect, also causes crossings (117 of 536 on seed 1002, none on 1001 in 10 Myr). Fixing only the
   correction leaves the onset intact.
3. **Thick crust is present, but broad, very high plateaus are scarce before the landscape begins** (high confidence
   in the shortfall, moderate in the explanation). Across 156 maps of A (every 10 Myr, six seeds), the largest
   connected area above 4 km was 0.492 Mkm² (Earth 2.100 today; C 0.387). On common 1° cells, A has *more*
   continental area at 60–65 km of total crust than Earth (1.06 against 0.84 Mkm²), yet stands at a median of 2,855 m
   there against Earth's 4,023. At 65–70 km: 0.36 against 0.92 Mkm², standing at 3,608 against 4,616 m. No seed has a
   majority-continental 1° cell at 70–80 km, where Earth has 0.81 Mkm². At 45–50 km, A is already *higher* (1,245
   against 708 m). So raising the datum or general thickening would push up the wrong part of the distribution. Two
   mechanisms bear on this. `crust_density_rule` 'pressure' treats densification as equilibrium through the whole
   lower-crust share, an explicit upper-bound assumption. `eclogite_rule` 'foundering' removes crystalline thickness
   beyond 65 km with τ 10 Myr. The test: pick the broadest sustained collision by its incoming continental volume and
   duration *before* looking at elevation. Then compare incoming flux, root growth, spreading, foundering and vertical
   support in that belt.
4. **The ocean has too much young floor and too little relief at comparable depth** (high confidence in the
   mismatches, moderate in their causes). Floor younger than 10 Myr: 47.4 against 29.9 Mkm². Production over the last
   100 Myr: 5.19 against 3.37 km²/yr, while ridge length matches (96,000 against 94,800 km). The first check is the
   integrated normal opening speed times ridge length, compared with the newly created area and the surviving age
   cohorts. Deep-floor roughness (39 against 77 m) is a separate problem. The 30–60 Myr mean depth is already within
   87 m of Earth's, so a further age–depth fit would not supply the missing variation.
5. **The meso loses mountain detail even on Earth input** (high confidence for the archived evaluation). The review
   disagrees with journal 146's statement that "about half" the planet's gap is the meso's: the two inputs have
   different distributions even within a class, so the ratios do not partition causes (§6).

#### 2. The review's lithosphere point, restated for the record

Journal 150 §5 read "the mean lithosphere age rises 5.5 Myr per Myr under C" as the cause of the low datum level.
The review's correction: the effective age comes from an inverse thickness table used as a clock. With the actual
function, 4 m of thickening on a 40 km column takes a 1,800 Myr effective age to 3,600 Myr while the table's L
changes by about 0.002 km. Material selection also changes an area mean. So mean age cannot say how far C pushes the
surface down. The measure is the change in L thickness, column mass and uplift on persistent parcels, split by cause:
mixing, mechanical strain, cooling and resets. Journal 150's concern stands: C does sit lower on the datum
(share under it 0.193 → 0.281).

#### 5. Claude's order inside the plate work (CLAUDE.md delegation), with reasons

1. **Pass 112: repair C's transfer, on a small physical example first.** Following the review's specification, a
   unit test before any run: rotate a normal-basement patch beside an accreted arc and remove a known forearc volume
   within the physical inputs' allowance. Require: local debit only against available arc material; requested and
   removed volumes both conserved, any shortfall booked, never taken from elsewhere; zero change on distant basement;
   real subaerial erosion tracked separately. Repeat with a newly exposed cell and an ownership change. The per-plate
   net correction (pass 109) must also stop moving untouched basement (§3). How depends on the trace: put the
   residual where the change was made, or book it. **No global clamp**: a clamp can create crust while passing the
   floor assertion. Then rerun `remap_trace.py` (10 steps on 1001 and 1002, and 250 on 1001; the target is zero
   crossings that are not booked), then the planet tests with C as the default, pinning the three trajectory checks
   of journal 150 §3 with notes. Then re-sweep C against the control with a new frozen prediction (template:
   `precheck-p111-confirm/prediction.md`) so that 2001–2006 run in the same sweep. The prediction must also predict the
   shallow-water share and the shore step (§7), since those are C's measured costs. Reason: this repairs a built
   candidate's integrity defect, and C moves every "where it is" measure toward Earth.
2. **Then the lithosphere, measured in km and m** (replaces "the thermal-age trace" of journal 150 §5). On persistent
   continental parcels, a per-step split of the change in L, column mass and uplift into mixing, mechanical strain,
   cooling and resets, for both remaps, with the rule written first. Effective age is reported only as a symptom.
3. **Then the plateau test (unparked).** "How high it is" is part of the target, and the review supplies a test that
   does not fit a statistic: elevation at equal crust thickness (60–65 km: 2,855 against 4,023 m) and the missing
   70–80 km roots. Pick the broadest sustained collision by incoming continental volume and duration before reading
   elevation. Book incoming flux, root growth, lateral spreading, foundering (`eclogite_thick_m` 65 km / τ 10 Myr) and
   the 'pressure' density's upper-bound assumption. It shares the vertical-support question with item 2, so it follows
   it. Not a Tibet per seed.
4. **The ocean basin**: integrated normal opening × ridge length against the created area and the surviving age
   cohorts, before touching ridge length. Within-age roughness separately, with no further age–depth fit.
5. **The long-run crust loss**: a separate lifetime constraint. Book collision, rifting and sediment-to-crust
   conversions by interval on the two 500 Myr series before any longer integration.
