**2. A correction to pass 45.** Its note "a dense sheet gives negative surface topography" was wrong for the code as
written: h = +σ_rr/(ρ g) gave a *rise* over a dense sheet. The isostatic limit fixes the sign (a sheet at the surface
must give h = −σ/ρ_m): the radial tension under a surface held flat pulls a free surface down, h = −σ_rr/(ρ_m g). The
velocity sign was right (the sheet sinks; the surface flow converges on it), so pass 45's traction numbers stand, and
its rms and spectra do not depend on the sign. U2 now tests the sign (−1 at the surface, isoviscous l = 2: −0.96 /
−0.40 / −0.09 at 100 / 1,500 / 2,500 km, → 0 at the CMB). Found before any planet run was read (a first slabs run with
the wrong sign was stopped at −220 Myr and discarded).

**5. α(z) (`slab_alpha` 'depth', now the slab rule's default).** A parcel keeps its mass and its temperature deficit;
its density anomaly is ρ α ΔT per volume and its volume scales as 1/ρ, so its mass anomaly scales with α alone:
factor α(z)/α_GDH1, with α_GDH1 = 3.1·10⁻⁵ K⁻¹, Stein & Stein's own value. Upper mantle: linear through Steinberger &
Calderwood's (2006, GJI 167, 1461) phase-boundary averages (2.378·10⁻⁵ at 400 km, 2.079·10⁻⁵ at 660 km). Lower mantle:
their form α₀ exp[−(δ_T/b)(1 − (ρ₀/ρ)^b)], δ_T 5.5 and b 1.4 (Anderson et al. 1992), along PREM's density, with its two
constants set by their stated 2.5·10⁻⁵ below 670 km and 1.0·10⁻⁵ at the base. So the factor is 0.83 at 300 km, 0.67 at
660, 0.81 below 670, 0.52 at 1,500 and 0.33 at 2,850 km. Earth's tomographic densities carry this decrease implicitly,
so 'depth' is the like-for-like comparison with the proxy; 'constant' stays as the pass's first variant.

**6. Why the amplitude is high (a check, not a tuning).**
- **Is the line estimate the planet's real consumption?** The pass-45 small item says the planet's rough boundaries
  carry ~0.625 h of line per node, not 0.796. Measured directly on the same steps (n = 217, cloud seed 1001, −230 …
  −180 Myr), the record's rate × line estimate against the oceanic plate nodes the overlap rule actually consumes:
  area 1.02–1.04×, mass 1.03–1.10×. So there is no overcount; the record carries what the plates consume.
- **The planet subducts more than Earth.** It consumed 2.5–2.7 km²/yr at −220 … −180 Myr. Its deposition over the last
  100 Myr is 3.2·10¹³ kg/yr, against Earth's ~2·10¹³ (~3–3.4 km²/yr of floor at a mean thermal mass of ~6·10⁶ kg/m²):
  ×1.6. That is the known subduction excess (contour subduction 73k against 51k km; intra-oceanic 2×).
- **Earth's tomographic proxy holds less than Earth's own subduction implies.** ~2·10¹³ kg/yr over ~200 Myr of record
  is ~4·10²¹ kg, ~2·10²¹ after α(z), against the proxy's 1.37·10²¹ (×1.5). Damping and finite resolution lower
  tomographic amplitudes, and the clip to the fast part drops slab material read as slow.
- Together: 1.6 × 1.5 ≈ 2.4, against the measured 2.8 (the rest is plausibly the planet's more coherent, mid-mantle-heavy
  record). D's per-mass response is not in question: the same kernels reproduce Earth's numbers exactly.
- So the amplitude failure says two things. The planet's slab flux is too large, a second consequence of B's
  subduction excess. And the proxy band may sit low for a record built from a subduction history. The band stays as
  fixed (no post-hoc change); the comparison with Earth's *observed* residual-topography spectrum (Valentine & Davies
  2020, in their normalization), owed since pass 45, is the right second reference and goes first next pass.
