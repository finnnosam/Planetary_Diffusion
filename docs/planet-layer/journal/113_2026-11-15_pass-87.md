**1. What the pass set out to do** (STATE §0, order item 2; journal 112 §4–5). The continents lose 3.9% of their

the rifting rise traced on the way.

**2. The arms, and why H 1,000 is added and H 300 is read only.** Under `trench_flux` 'consumed' each trench term is
fed the oceanic area the trench actually consumed, so `arc_H_ocean_m` is what Earth's measurement is: arc crust added
per m² of ocean consumed. Journal 57 derived it from Earth alone: Dimalanta et al. 2002's 30–95 km³/Myr per km of
intra-oceanic trench over Bird 2003's intra-oceanic closing rate (61.5 mm/yr, length-weighted on 20.8k km) = 490–1,540 m,
midpoint 1,000 (× Earth's 1.28 km²/yr consumed = 1.3 km³/yr, against Scholl & von Huene's 1.5). The 300 m of today
is no measurement of that flux: it stood beside the band law, which counts node-typed trench at 1.7–2.7× the
consumed area, so today's arcs grow 15 km³/Myr per km at H 300. The audit's condition ("not to change before the
intra-oceanic trench is fixed") was written for the band law, whose arcs scale with trench length; under 'consumed'
they scale with the area consumed. So:
