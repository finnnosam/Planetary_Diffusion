**2. Seed 1001, ocean arm: Pangea whole for 100 Myr.** The arm has 5 rifts against the control's 28, 16 plates at 0 Myr,
53.6k km of ridge and rifted production 550 km/Myr (control 1,978). The one difference before it is the initiation at
−173 Myr (4.1 Mkm² of old floor offshore instead of a 104 Mkm² ocean along a coast, on an oceanic plate carrying a
sliver of continent); afterwards the Pangea plate (195 Mkm², 194 of them continent) does not split until −73 Myr, in
the control it splits at −162. The K rule (`_split_events_earth`) draws one candidate line per plate per step and
splits at rate prod × Ac / L (L the margin the line would make, both sides). A probe (cloud, seed 1009, both arms,
130 Myr, `precheck-p68-craton/probe_split.py`) logs it: a cut across all of Pangea makes L ≈ 44,000–49,000 km (a
wiggled line counted on both sides), so before −170 Myr (Earth's quiet Pangea, 1–5 km/Myr/Mkm²) the whole plate's
rate is 0.006–0.015 per Myr, and at the breakup peak 0.05–0.09: 2–3 expected splits per 20 Myr. A 97 Myr stall at that
rate is a ~10⁻³ event unless that seed's lines run longer; the initiation site does not enter the rate (the plate that
stalled carries no old floor). So it is a property of K on the whole start: a whole supercontinent is cut rarely and
then in large pieces, each cut paying for its full length at once; Earth's breakup propagated rift by rift. Scored, not
a veto; a watch-point and a candidate (rift segments shorter than a whole-plate cut) for K, after the land's height.
