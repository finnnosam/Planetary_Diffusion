### Run speed (21 Nov, after pass 99, at the user's request): the split candidates cost a fifth of a step; an exact screen and plate-local arrays make a run ~19% faster with identical results; memory per run measured; scheduling left as is

The user asked whether runs could be faster (the Placed Field Sweep was running). Measured, not guessed: the sweep
timing record on the device and cProfile / line_profiler on cloud runs (n = 217, threads 1).

#### 1. Where a sweep's time goes

- **Scheduling.** The Marine Diffusion Sweep (12 runs, 8 workers × 2 threads) ran in two waves: 8 runs at 26–30 min
  each, then 4 runs at 20 min on half-idle cores (sweep 49 min of 57; tests 4.5 min, reviews 3.6 min). Throughput
  at 8 concurrent runs 0.28 runs/min, at 4 only 0.20: the machine is throughput-bound, so the tail is the waste.
- **Memory per run** (cloud, Linux, threads 1, seed 1007): RSS 0.70 GB at 10 plates, 0.98 GB at 20, 1.3–1.5 GB at
  30–34 plates. On the user's machine 23 of 32 GB were in use with 8 runs going, so 12 at once would need roughly
  6 GB more: too close. Workers stay at 8. (For an 18-run sweep, 9 workers would give two waves instead of three.)
- **A step** costs ~2.9 s early (10 plates) and ~6.4 s late (30 plates). At 21–30 plates (line_profiler, 6 steps
  from pickled states of the seed-1007 run): split events 22–32% of a step, erosion 21%, raster update 16–20%,
  crust flow 11%, claims 6–8%, `_world_from` 5–7%.

#### 2. The split candidates

`_split_events_earth` draws a candidate rift line every step for every plate with continent (the rate needs its
margin length): ~8 calls of `_chord_line` per step. Each tries up to ~190 candidate cuts and labels the connected
pieces of the whole plate for every one (`components` on the plate, then `bincount`): 7,036 tries in 6 steps, of
which 84 reached the tension test. Debugging the rejected ones (`dbg.py`): almost all are "one huge piece and crumbs"
(e.g. 85.8 Mkm² and 52 pieces of ≤ 0.03), rejected because the second-largest piece is below `piece_min_area_mkm2`.

Built (no model change):

- **`graphops.two_big_pieces`**, an exact screen run before the full labelling: the unblocked nodes beside the cut
  are grouped into strips (each lies in one piece), one breadth-first fill grows from all strips at once, classes
  that meet are merged (union-find), and a class whose frontier empties holds its whole piece. It returns False as
  soon as at most one class can still reach the floor (the exact labelling then rejects the cut too), True otherwise
  (the caller labels exactly). A piece counts as small only below floor × (1 − 1e-9), so summation order cannot turn
  a piece the exact count calls big into a small one. It sees only pieces that touch the cut, so `_chord_line` uses
  it only when at most one piece of the uncut plate could reach the floor and the nucleus lies in it (the plate's
  other pieces are crumbs that stay whole). On 1,352 screened cuts it never disagreed with the exact labelling and
  rejected 99% of them.
- `_chord_line` builds its cost arrays (line strength, wiggle, belt) on the plate's own rows only: the same numbers
  element by element; rows of other nodes are never read (every edge leaving the plate is closed). The random draws
  are unchanged (the wiggle field is still drawn for the whole graph). The cost buffer is kept between calls.
- `_split_margin_km` counts on the plate's own rows (the same count).
- The force features are computed once per `_chord_line` call (`_force_order` keeps them for `_cut_tension`).

`_chord_line`: 7.4 → 3.1 s over the 6 profiled steps (30 plates).

#### 3. Identity and timing

- n = 48, seed 1007, 70 steps, threads 1, `state()` before and after every step: 71 of 71 states identical to the
  pass-99 code.
- n = 217 from the pickled seed-1007 state at step 120 (21 plates): 25 steps identical (469 candidate lines drawn);
  from step 200 (30 plates): 12 steps identical.
- **Full length**: n = 217, seed 1025, 250 Myr, threads 1, old and new side by side on the cloud's two cores, md5 per
  step of elevation, crust, sediment, rock, the flag, plate ids, the field, the plates' motions and the event list:
  **250 of 250 steps identical**, the crust and sediment ledgers identical. Run time 1,270 → 1,028 s (−19%); steps
  201–250 6.38 → 4.97 s (−22%). Clean single-process benchmark from step 200: median step 6.42 → 5.12 s.
- Cloud `PLANET_TESTS` (39 files): 252 passed, 2 skipped. New `tests/test_split_screen.py` (in `PLANET_TESTS`): the
  screen never rejects a cut the exact count accepts (random plates, crumbs, cuts and floors), and `_chord_line`
  gives the same cut with the screen and with it forced off; 2 passed on the device too.

Expected on the user's machine: a 12-run sweep's runs ~49 → ~40 min (throughput-bound, so the per-run gain carries).
To be read on the next sweep's `timings.txt`.

#### 4. Not done (left as options)

- The per-plate nearest-node lookups in `_claims` and `_update_rasters` (~17% of a late step): exact restriction to
  the plate's own area needs a proof that no other raster node can map into it; not worth the risk now.
- Running the tests (4.5 min) alongside the runs: a change to `run_sweep`'s failure handling; ~4 min per sweep.
- Changing `threads`: it moves results at 1e-12 and breaks the identity chain against earlier sweeps. Not done.
