**1. Geoid kernels** (`planet_tectonics.mantle_flow`; default path unchanged: `FlowKernels` is built only with
`dyn_rule` 'slabs', and its KH, KV are untouched).
- `FlowKernels.KC[l, j]` = +σ_rr(CMB)/g per unit sheet: the CMB deflection's mass. The core is a hydrostatic fluid, so
  its pressure at the CMB radius has no l ≥ 1 part and the boundary moves by σ_rr/(Δρ_c g); a dense sheet pushes it
  down (KC → −1 for a sheet at the CMB, → 0 at the surface), as KH → −1 at the surface.
- `geoid_kernels()` / `geoid()`: without self-gravitation, N_l = 4πG_N a/((2l+1)g) [(r/a)^(l+2) + KH + KC (c/a)^(l+2)],
  each sheet's exterior potential. With `self_grav`, the deflections are measured from the perturbed equipotentials
  (m_a = KH σ + ρ_top N(a), m_c = KC σ + ρ_core N(c), a 2 × 2 solve per degree): the boundary part of
  self-gravitation in a mantle of uniform density (the reduced pressure p + ρ₀φ absorbs the rest). A bound, not
  PREM's stratification. `RHO_CORE_JUMP` 4,340 kg/m³ (PREM), `GRAV_CONST` 6.674·10⁻¹¹; uniform g 9.81 as the kernels.
- **Verified** (`precheck-p50-geoid/cmp_geoid.out`): the collocation reference of pass 48 (`tests/test_flow_reference`)
  now also returns σ_rr at the CMB. Every degree 1–31 at the 51 load depths: `sh08` KC 1.1·10⁻⁷, KH 4.3·10⁻⁷, geoid
  kernel 1.8·10⁻⁶ of each degree's maximum. Isoviscous: 5.3·10⁻⁴ at the reference's N = 16, 1.6·10⁻⁷ at N = 32 (the
  reference's own resolution at l = 31; `cmp_iso_N.out`). Limits: sheets at either boundary give KH or KC = −1 and no
  geoid. Signs: isoviscous gives a negative geoid over a dense sheet at every depth (Hager 1984: "always negative");
  a lower mantle 30× stiffer makes it positive at 1,000 km for l = 2 and 4. The self-gravitating solve with zero
  contrasts is the plain kernel. New test `test_geoid_kernels_limits_signs_and_self_gravitation_off`; `_check` now
  also asserts KC. Cloud and device: 12 passed (`test_flow_reference`, `test_mantle_flow`).

- With `sh08`, in Hager's slab band (l 4–9), the geoid asks for 0.48× E3's mass and the observed topography for 0.43×.
  The tomography holds about half as well (E3 / proxy 1.6–2.3 by depth bin, journal 70; E1's proxy through `sh08` gives
  222 m, on the observation). **Three Earth witnesses (residual topography, the geoid's slab band, the tomographic mass)
  agree: Earth's present-day slab buoyancy is about half of what D's record holds.** At l 2–3 the geoid asks for more
  than the slabs give (1.55): part of Earth's degree-2–3 geoid is usually attributed to the LLSVPs' upwellings, which a slab-only load does not have.
- The literature saw the same: LR98's own slab model, with its geoid-fitting profile, gave dynamic topography "rather
  large" (maps to −3 km), and Ricard et al. 1993's ~1–2 km lows; Steinberger et al. 2019 find degree 2 predicted more
  than 2× the observation.
- What D's record holds: each parcel carries the plate's whole thermal mass, (ρ_m − ρ_w)(GDH1(age) − GDH1(0)), times
  α(z)/α_GDH1, conserved to 2,850 km. Candidates for the missing half, each to be taken from measurement, not chosen to
  make 0.45: the slab's compositional buoyancy (crust and harzburgite by depth; journal 70's ~15% estimate is too small
  alone); α at slab temperatures (the cold slab's mean α below the ambient α(z)); the lower-mantle sinking rate and
  residence (12 mm/yr; but the excess is spread over the depth bins, 1.6–2.3×, so residence alone is unlikely); the
  observation's own range (0.15–0.30 km). The planet carries the same per-parcel mass, so whatever Earth needs applies
  to the planet unchanged; its own excess (1.41×, the flux) stays B's.
