#### 2. The repair, and how it got there

The old return (pass 108, corrected in pass 109) mixed each world cell's *net* increment onto the plate nodes its overlap
covers, then added one per-plate, per-crust-type offset so the net volume matched. Both halves of the defect follow from
that (journal 151 §3):

- a forearc cut on a world cell whose overlap mixes a 12 km arc node with 7 km basement nodes went onto all of them, so
  the basement went below 7 km (the forearc law limits the cut to the *cell's* excess, not each contributor's);
- the offset moved basement no process touched that step.

The return as committed (`_tent_return`, version 3):

1. A world cell's increment goes to the plate nodes it read (its contributors), owned by the plate or not, as under
   'nearest', where the read nodes take the increments. A live owned node that no weight reaches takes the world node
   over it, as before.
2. Each increment is split into its gain and its loss **before** mixing, so a cut next to a fill cannot cancel to a
   near-zero that a rescaling then amplifies (pass 109's blow-up came from splitting after mixing).
3. Gains go back by the overlap weights, as before.
4. A cell's loss is debited from its contributors in proportion to weight × the material each has above its floor
   (`_return_floor`: oceanic thickness keeps its 7 km basement, the forearc law's own floor; continental thickness,
   sediment and rock may go to zero), and never more than all of it. A contributor with nothing above its floor takes
   nothing. Since a forearc cut is at most the cell's excess, which is at most the weighted sum of its contributors'
   excesses, no node goes below its floor by construction.
5. Each plate's gains and its losses, per crust type, are closed to what its nodes can take by rescaling **only the
   nodes that took that sign** (no uniform offset). The factor corrects the overlap areas' mismatch and stays near one.
6. A loss beyond a node's material after the rescaling, or beyond a cell's material, is booked
   (`erosion_totals['return_shortfall_<field>_m3']`), as is a gain no node can take (`return_unplaced_<field>_m3`).
   No new number.

Two earlier versions failed on the way, each caught by a check written before it ran.

- **Version 1** gave increments only to the nodes the plate still owns (as the pass 108 code did) and closed the whole
  world volume by the factor. It passed R1–R5 (§3), and the Transfer Sweep was started on it at 17:25. In that sweep
  **seed 1004 under C went non-finite at −134 Myr**: sea level NaN, land 0% from −130 Myr to the end; run.log warnings
  from `crust_kernels.py:53` and `crust.py:302`. `nan_probe.py` replayed it (it matches the sweep's snapshots once run
  as the sweep runs: `threads` 2, `NUMBA_NUM_THREADS` 2, `state()` after every step; without those it does not, and
  the first replay found nothing). A continental cell's 102 m gain had only one contributor, which the plate no longer
  owned. The two owned continental nodes of that plate had denormal gains (~10⁻³⁰⁸ m), so the factor was
  1.7 × 10¹¹ m³ / 8 × 10⁻²⁹⁹ m³ = ∞. The same sweep's parent process also ended unseen at about 17:54, after collecting
  two runs: its terminal tab was gone and the borrow step and the reviews never ran. The cause is not known; nothing in
  the folder says, and the workers finished their runs. The sweep is void: renamed `sweep-20261127-173125-void`, nothing
  in it read for terrain. The prediction (§4) had been frozen before it started and is unchanged.
- **Version 2** renormalised each cell's weights over its owned contributors, booking the part no owned node could hold.
  The infinite factor was gone (a unit test for the case was added), but **R3 failed**: over 250 Myr the booked
  thickness shortfall was 0.15% of the returned loss on seed 1001 and 0.11% on 1004 (band 0.1%), plus 0.6 / 0.4 Mkm³ of
  unplaced gain. `short_split.py` (60 steps on 1001) put 90% of the booked loss on cells with no owned contributor:
  half of it where every contributor was hidden, half where the contributors sat over new crust. Those are nodes the
  world reads and shows, so excluding them was the fault, not the cap. Version 3 is item 1 above. R1's
  ownership-change clause ("its share goes to the other contributors or is booked") was written for version 1's
  design. It is replaced by "the unowned contributor takes its share by its material; nothing onto basement; nothing
  short", a change made after a failed band was traced, and recorded as such.

#### 7. Decision

- **C is adopted as a numerics fix**: `remap_rule` 'overlap' and `rift_area` 'plates' are PARAMS. This follows pass 111's
  rule, fixed in the sweep's header before the runs: it passes every veto on both seed sets, and the oceanic basement
  invariant now holds on every snapshot. The two default-pin tests are updated, the old values added to
  `crust_review.PREDATING`, and the whole test suite run with the new defaults: 384 of 384 pass (`pytest_all_C_default.txt`).
- **No terrain claim.** The frozen prediction failed on the land fraction, so this pass does not claim that C improves
  the map. The failed line is the next problem (CLAUDE.md: an arm adopted even if a score gets worse makes that score
  the next thing to work on). How much land is part of the target, and it went away from Earth here.
- **Next (pass 113): why the continents stand lower under C, measured in km and m** (STATE item 2, now with a target
  symptom). Land −2.4 points, and the share of thick crust under the datum 0.25 → 0.34, on the confirmation seeds. On
  persistent continental parcels, split the change in lithosphere thickness L, column mass and uplift into mixing,
  mechanical strain, cooling and resets, under both remaps, with the rule written first. The new default is C, so the
  'nearest' arm is now the comparison. The candidate fixes named in journal 150 §5 (the thermal age read at the nearest
  plate node under 'overlap'; a thickening rule that keeps the age on Lamb's flat stretches) wait for that split.
