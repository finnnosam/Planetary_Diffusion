**3. The mechanism built** (`planet_tectonics.crust_flow`; values all measured or assumed, none fitted to the planet):
- *Geotherm*: steady conduction in each column from its crust T and C's lithosphere L. Heat production 1.0 µW/m³ in
  the felsic part, 0.4 in the lower crust (`lower_crust_share` 0.324; Hasterok & Chapman 2011: upper crust, granulite
  terranes); a 40 km crust's bulk is then 0.81, inside Jaupart et al. 2016's 0.65–0.87. The base of the lithosphere at
  1,175 °C, the isotherm Hoggard et al. 2020 map as L (the planet's Lamb frame is built on their L). Conductivity 2.5
  (crust) / 3.0 (mantle) W/m/K and 10 °C at the surface, assumed. Thickening by pure shear carries the sources down.
  Check: a 40 km crust over 178 km gives a surface flux of 47.5 mW/m² and a Moho flux of 15.3, Moho at 471 °C (Earth's
  cratons ~40–45 and 10–15 mW/m², Jaupart & Mareschal 1999); 45 / 50 / 55 / 60 / 70 km over 140 km: Moho 606 / 678 /
  749 / 817 / 946 °C; 40 km over 100 km 648 °C. Steady state is the upper bound (a thickened column heats over
  ~20–100 Myr).
- *Rheology*: dislocation creep, wet quartzite above (Gleason & Tullis 1995, melt-free: A 1.1·10⁻⁴ MPa⁻⁴ s⁻¹, n 4,
  Q 223 kJ/mol), wet anorthite in the lower crust (Rybacki & Dresen 2000: log A 2.6 MPa⁻³ s⁻¹, n 3, Q 356 kJ/mol),
  simple shear in tensor form, γ̇ = 3^((n+1)/2) A τⁿ exp(−Q/RT).
- *Driving stress*: the isostatic surface slope, τ(d) = ρ_c g S (d − d₀), S = (1 − ρ_c/ρ_m) |∇T|. The flux per unit
  width q = |∫ d γ̇ dd|; the edge's diffusivity q / |∇T| (+ kappa_normal 30, kept). Two boundary modes (Rey,
  Vanderhaeghe & Teyssier 2001): **'channel'**, fixed boundary: the upper crust is held by the plate around it, zero
  velocity at the surface and the Moho, d₀ where the shear integrates to zero (Poiseuille with depth-dependent
  viscosity); **'sheet'**, free boundary: the crust spreads from its free surface down on a fixed Moho (d₀ = 0); and
  **'rey'**, each where Rey et al. put it: fixed within 290 km of active convergence (the arc belt over a converging
  trench, collision boundaries closing faster than `boundary_min_mm_yr`; half of Earth's collisional thickening lies
  within 290 km of the boundary, `crust_kernels.COLLISION_KERNEL`, measured), free elsewhere.
- *Numerics*: q tabulated once per run in (T 10–100 km, L 40–320 km, log₁₀ S −6 … −0.5), interpolated trilinearly in
  log q on every edge, each edge once (exactly symmetric, conservative). Explicit steps; edges above
  `flow_kappa_split_m2_yr` (2·10³) take their own substeps inside steps of at most 0.1 Myr (operator split): on a
  bumpy n = 217 state it is as close to a 10× finer explicit run as the plain explicit step (rms 58 against 55 m) and
  ~5× faster. Ceiling `flow_kappa_max_m2_yr` 2·10⁵ (today's collapse ceiling; a 300 km edge relaxes in ~0.5 Myr),
  the share of edges at it booked.

tuned on, is held in by ongoing convergence (the fixed mode), while the planet's thick crust is mostly post-collisional
(98% collided, kept for hundreds of Myr: journal 75), where the free mode applies.

What it shows, against §6's expectation written before it: **the free mode does not thin the tail; it flattens it.**
'sheet' removes the > 65 km crust and lowers the highest node by 1.1 km, but its plateaus stand at 45–55 km (0.217
against 0.128) over 145 km of lithosphere and > 45 km rises to 0.258. The reason is the power law: q ~ Sⁿ with n 3–4, so
once a plateau's top is flat its flow stops whatever its thickness, and its edges stay steep (Tibet's form); the
fitted law's diffusivity depends on thickness alone and keeps spreading a flat top, so its plateaus thin and widen. The
lithosphere under the thick crust is thick (pure-shear 'thicken', bounded at 177.7 km), which keeps the Moho at
< 800 °C on all but 0.7% of the continent under 'sheet'. 'rey' pins the belts (19% of the continent within 290 km of
