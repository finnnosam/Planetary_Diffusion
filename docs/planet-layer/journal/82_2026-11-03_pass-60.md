### Sixtieth pass (3 Nov): the area deficit measured on combo-02 (the no-flow base). It is not an area-rate problem: the planet's collisions remove area at half Earth's present rate (0.22 against 0.43 Mkm²/Myr, Bird's CCB) and its rifts add it at a third to two-thirds of Earth's; its net (−0.08) equals Earth's present boundary net. The deficit (27 Mkm²) is ~70% volume (the planet ends 9.6% below Earth's crust volume: it starts 3.7% above and loses 12.8%) and ~30% thickness (mean 38.4 against 36.6 km). The volume loss (−5.0 km³/yr against Earth's 0 … −1.8, Clift et al. 2009) is mostly the sediment route: 5.6 km³/yr of detritus leaves the continental crust (3.7 subducted, Earth 1.65), and the planet's ocean floor holds 1.8× Earth's sediment, 6.7× near trenches and 2.7× beyond 1,000 km, with no continental rise (floor 0.25–0.85 km deeper than Earth's within 200 km of passive edges). The low sea is set at the start on this base (−285 m at 250 Myr, −297 at 0). Next: S, a margin wedge (slope and rise) for the sediment that overflows the shelf, with its test set; no code changed

Bird's CRB is not all continental: its largest terms are back-arc spreading in the south-west Pacific (Okinawa,
Andaman, Havre, Manus, Lau and others: ~0.15 of the 0.36); East Africa (SO-AF, 4,947 km at 2.9 mm/yr) gives 0.015, Baikal
(AM-EU) 0.025. GSRM near subduction zones is mostly interseismic elastic shortening (locked megathrusts); left out,
Earth's continents today lose area in deformation at ~0.5 Mkm²/Myr net (India–Asia, Arabia, the Andes' back-arc).
**Reading:** the planet's collisions remove area at about half Earth's present rate and its rifts add it at a third
(Bird) to two-thirds (GSRM) of Earth's; its net equals Earth's present boundary net. Earth's present deformation does
not balance either: today is a collisional time, and the long-run balance is not readable from rates. Pass 53's
"collisions turn area into thickness and nothing returns it" holds, but the rate ledger does not name a process the
planet runs too fast or lacks. So the deficit is read from the end state.

**3. Where Earth's thin crust lies, and where the planet's does** (`earth_thin`, `earth_thin_where`,
`earth_bins_by_type`, `planet_thin`, `thin_z_by_T`). Earth's 20–30 km crust: 36.9 Mkm² (0.189 of the continent).
By CRUST1.0 type: shelf 9.8, slope / transition 11.3, thickened oceanic crust (B-, W- plateaus 4.2; oceanic types
with ≥ 20 km, mostly hotspot tracks and aseismic ridges, 4.6), arcs 2.6, extended 2.1, cratons and platforms 2.0,
orogens 0.4; 85% under the sea; 4.4 Mkm² in the ice regions. The planet's: 15.7 Mkm² (1.45 of it on oceanic
flags). By elevation, Earth / planet (Mkm²): below −2,000 m 3.4 / 2.7; −2,000 … −200 17.3 / 7.8; −200 … 0 (the shelf)
10.6 / 1.3; 0 … 500 4.3 / 2.6; above 1.4 / 1.3. By distance from any continental edge: 0–150 km 26.4 / 14.2; 150–500
7.9 / 1.1; beyond 2.7 / 0.3.
At the same thickness the planet stands where Earth does (20–22.5 km: −1,652 against −1,324 m; 27.5–30: −199 / −62;
30–32.5: +47 / +27; the gates' −100 … −330 m offset): **the missing shelf is the thickness distribution, not
isostasy.** Earth has 1.5–2.8× the planet's area in every 2.5 km bin from 20 to 32.5 km (6.7 / 6.0 / 6.2 / 13.6 / 16.6
against 3.5 / 3.4 / 4.0 / 4.8 / 7.6 Mkm²) and less at 35–40 (52.1 / 67.5). Earth's surplus at 20–25 km is mostly
thickened oceanic crust (6.5 of 12.7), a process the planet does not have; at 27.5–32.5 km it is shelf (11.8),
arcs (4.0), extended crust (3.8) and slope (3.3).
**The start has none of it** (`thin_history` on the freeze run, seed 1001): at −250 Myr the planet's 20–30 km crust
is 0.0195 of the surface against Earth's 0.072, and 35–40 km 0.193 against 0.122. `start_crust` earth spreads
Earth's *stable* crust (Precambrian and platform types far from boundaries) over the whole supercontinent, so it
starts with Earth's area and 3.7% more than Earth's volume but without margins, shelves or arcs. The run doubles the
thin crust (0.040 at 0 Myr on the freeze run) by rifting.

**4. The volume budget against Earth's** (`volume_ledger`; `crust_budget` printout of the sweep). combo-02, whole
run, km³/yr: continental net −5.04 = crystalline erosion −8.42 + trench on continents (arc − subduction erosion)
+0.32 + sediment accreted +0.55 + collision sediment +1.42 + volcanic +0.13 + arcs turned continental +0.64 + arcs
docked +0.41. Ocean-floor sediment: +5.58 from the continents, −3.66 subducted, −0.55 accreted, +0.99 stock growth.
Earth (Clift, Vannucchi & Morgan 2009, abstract): sediment subduction 1.65, subduction erosion 1.3, lower-crust
delamination ~1.1, collision 0.4, dissolved 0.4 km³/yr; arc addition ~3.8 for steady state, as low as 2.0 if the
Cenozoic crust is losing < 1.8 km³/yr; "rough equilibrium during the Phanerozoic".
Term by term, the planet's net loss exceeds Earth's by ~3.2–5 km³/yr. The sediment route alone is ~3: what leaves
the continents and is subducted or parked on the floor (4.65 against Earth's ~1.65, which includes pelagic sediment).
The arcs give ~1.5 against Earth's net ~0.7–2.5 (addition minus subduction erosion), about Earth's. The planet has
no delamination or collision loss (−1.5 on Earth), which is in its favour. **The sediment route is ≥ 60% of the
excess volume loss.**

it is set at the start (water below the datum 2,825 m against Earth's 2,629; the start's deep floor 4,385 m on

**6. Design: candidate S, the margin wedge** (Claude's decision by the goal and the rules: reversible, inside item
1's agreed direction, written here).
- Why S first: the volume is 70% of the deficit, the sediment route ≥ 60% of the excess volume loss, and the planet
  lacks the rise Earth has. The other pieces are smaller or need a new system: thickened oceanic crust (8.8 Mkm² of
  Earth's thin crust, a plume / LIP mechanism the planet does not have; 33% of the thin shortfall), the start's
  missing margins (an input), the thick tail (R).
- The mechanism: sediment that overflows the graded shelf is deposited in front of where it left the shelf up to the
  margin's equilibrium profile before any remainder runs to the sea-floor lows. Earth's turbidites and contourites
  build the slope and rise beside their source (Heezen, Tharp & Ewing 1959; the rise ~1:100 to 1:700, to ~4–5 km).
  The profile is Earth's measured one on this grid (section 4: p50 depth by distance from the passive edge, linear
  between bin centres, to 1,000 km; deeper floor beyond is filled from the bottom as now): a measured geometry at the
  grid spacing it runs at, used as the deposition ceiling (capacity), not as a test. Plate-layer only: an option in
  `surface_step` / `marine_sediment_kept` passed through `surface.py`; the landscape stage keeps its rule. Default off
  until a sweep adopts it; the default path stays identical step by step (n = 48).
- Test set (Earth on the same grid; bounds are upper bounds where Earth's number includes pelagic sediment the
  planet does not make):
  S1 ocean-floor sediment ≤ 192 Mkm³ (Earth's, with pelagic).
  S2 share of it beyond 1,000 km from the continent ≤ 0.14 (Earth 26 / 192, with pelagic).
  S3 mean sediment within 150 km of a trench ≤ 0.75 km (Earth 9 / 12, with pelagic).
  S4 sediment subducted, last 40 Myr, per km of trench (contour) ≤ Earth's 32 km³/Myr per km (Clift's 1.65 km³/yr
  over Bird's 51.5k km of SUB).
  Build checks: on a flat test sea floor the wedge reaches the profile from the source outward and every m³ is
  placed (ledger); with supply larger than the wedge holds, the excess fills the lows as now.
  Outcome readings, reported against the control with what each means fixed with the `.cmd`: continental volume
  change, label-free area, crust < 30 km, sea level, deep floor, the margin bathymetry by distance.
  Guards as the last sweep: pytest; elevation gates ±300 m on 6 of 6; volume ≥ −14%; export 4–8 km³/yr (reported:
  combo-02 fails it); margins; ledgers; `plate_pieces_review`'s B-b decision.
- What S is expected not to do (written before it runs): sediment kept on the rise stays on oceanic crust and counts
  as continental only once accreted, docked in a collision or thick enough (≥ 20 km). If S brings S1–S4 to Earth's
  and the volume does not move, the volume's next term is accommodation on the continental crust itself (Earth keeps
  320 Mkm³ there, much of it on thin margins that the planet lacks from the start), then the start's crust.
- Pass 61: build S (code, tests, identity), the cloud pre-check on seed 1010 with its control (combo-02's settings),
  and `Margin Wedge Sweep.cmd` with the rule fixed before the runs.
