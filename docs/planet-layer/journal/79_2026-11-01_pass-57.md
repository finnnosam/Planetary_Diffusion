**3. Earth under the same law** (`p57_earthterm`; ETOPO land without ice sheets, CRUST1.0 nearest 1° cell, the planet's
Airy line).
- The term from CRUST1.0's type ages, coast → interior: **−238 m** (area means; medians step +301 → −216 m, because the
  class ages are discrete). Earth's z − Airy over the same span: −675 m (means), −773 m (medians). The law carries ~35%.
- Crustal density by type (CRUST1.0's layer densities weighted by thickness: 2,813–2,825 kg/m³ in every band) does not
  carry the rest: with it the fall is −747 m (means), −764 (medians).
- The coast band is half an artefact. A land node at the coast reads a 1° cell that is partly sea (thinner crust → low
  Airy line → z − Airy high). On land nodes whose 1° cell is ≥ 90% land (31% of the coast band, all of the interior):
  coast z − Airy +407 → +158 m (medians), C1 −773 → **−524 m** (means −407). From 50–150 km inland the fall is −593 m
  (medians), so most of it is real. The term on those cells: +14 → −139 m (−153).
- So, measured on Earth: about −400 … −600 m of fall below the Airy line from coast to interior, of which the measured
  thermal-age law gives ~150–240 m. The rest is not the crust's density by type. Candidates, not yet measured: Earth's
  continental dynamic topography (interiors over old slabs), CRUST1.0's interior thickness errors, a lithosphere density
  contrast larger than the law's net 30 kg/m³ for the oldest roots.
