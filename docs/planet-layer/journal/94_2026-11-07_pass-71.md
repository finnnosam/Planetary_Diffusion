**5. Why the planet makes no fragments.** `_rift_line` draws seed a anywhere in the plate and seed b among the 10% of
the plate farthest from a; the line is the boundary of their cost-distance regions. Every split cuts its plate roughly in
half, across the whole continent: never a sliver, a microcontinent or an embayment. And the belt term
(`suture_weakness` 0.6 lowers an edge's cost by (1 − (w_a + w_b)/2)) works against its comment ("prefers old sutures"):
where one front crosses a cheap zone fast, the two fronts meet beyond it; the meeting line lands where cost is *high*.
(1D: seeds at 0 and 1, a zone [0.3, 0.4] at relative cost c: the fronts meet at (1.1 − 0.1c)/2 for c < 3 — 0.53 for
the as-built belts' c = 0.4, i.e. farther from the zone than with no zone at all — and inside it, at 0.35 + 0.15/c,
only for c ≥ 3.) Earth's continents deform and break where their lithosphere is weak:
elastic thickness > 100 km in cratons and < 40 km in Phanerozoic belts and along most margins, tracking the mantle's
seismic velocity at 100 km, and > 70% of shallow seismicity and volcanism where it is < 40 km (Audet & Bürgmann 2011,
Nature Geosci. 4, 184, doi:10.1038/ngeo1080); the Atlantic opened along old belts (Buiter & Torsvik 2014, Gondwana Res.
26, 627). The planet carries that weakness already: C's thermal thickness L(t) (Artemieva 2006), young at the coast
(56–67 Myr within 50 km) and in the belts, 1.0–1.7 Gyr inland.
