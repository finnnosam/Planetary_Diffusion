### Ninety-second pass (18 Nov): back-arc extension built (`backarc_extension`, off) with a trench law (`rollback_rule` 'fixed' / 'lallemand'); default path identical step by step; seed-1019 full-length pre-check of all three back-arc arms passes every veto reading; `Back Arc Sweep.cmd` ready (4 arms × 6 seeds)

**1. The build** (journal 118 §6's candidate, built as fixed there). `margin_shortening`'s rate is the upper plate's trenchward speed minus the trench's rollback, u − r (Heuret & Lallemand 2005; Lallemand et al. 2008: upper-plate deformation = its velocity − the trench's). The shortening keeps `max(0, u − r)`. With `backarc_extension` True, where an overriding trench node on continental crust has u − r < 0, the upper plate extends at e = r − u. Design question (a), where the stretched volume goes in a rigid raster: the arc–forearc block steps trenchward. Each step the overriding plate claims the oceanic node in front of the trench (the neighbour on the other plate most nearly along the trench normal) with probability e · dt · ℓ / a_f (ℓ = `BOUNDARY_NODE_H` · h, the trench length per node; a_f the front node's area; drawn from the run's own stream [seed, 10], so the default path draws nothing). The same area opens behind the arc as pure shear of that segment's back-arc band (nodes of the same plate whose nearest overriding trench node is the segment's, continental, thicker than `breakup_thick_m`). Each band node thins as T exp(−c), c = a_f k(d) / Σ a k (the rift's pure-shear form), never below 10 km. The band gives its volume to the new node, which keeps the edge node's crust, orogeny and thermal ages and carries no sediment. The front's raster node must be new to the plate and touch it. Ledger `backarc` (volume) and area ledger `backarc`. The stretched lithosphere thins with the crust (`stretched_age`, as rifts). The other option, a back-arc split of the overriding plate, was not built: the force law cannot give a forearc microplate its rollback motion. The steps happen in whole nodes (46 km), so the band thins in lumps of ~a_f / (ℓ W) ≈ 15% strain behind one trench node every few Myr, spread along strike. A run books `backarc_steps`, `backarc_stretched_m3`, `backarc_area_m2`, `backarc_expected_m2` and `backarc_capped_steps`.

Every run, back-arc on or off, now books (output only) the overriding trench length summed over steps: all, on continental crust, and of that shortening or extending (beyond 0 and beyond 5 mm/yr), plus oceanic upper plates extending (`overriding_*` in the erosion totals). These give the regime split against Earth's transects.

**2. (b) The trench law.** Earth's measurement is Lallemand et al. 2008 (Tectonics 27, TC3014), 166 transects at 2°. Their Table 2: mean trench motion is rollback 0.9 ± 3.0 cm/yr in SB04 (NNR 1.1 ± 3.0, HS3 0.6 ± 4.0), and 30% of trenches advance in SB04. Neutral arcs lie on Vup = 0.24 Vsub − 1.4 cm/yr in SB04 (NNR 0.32 Vsub − 1.9; HS3 0.5 Vsub − 2.3). Their Table 1 lists each transect's strain class. The text extraction lost the signs, so the classes were assigned by region from the literature (Andaman, Ryukyu, Marianas, Izu, S Kuril, Sandwich, Kermadec, Tonga, New Britain extending; Luzon, Philippines, Japan, the Andes from Colombia to Juan Fernández, the New Hebrides compressing; the rest neutral): 44 / 86 / 36 of 166 extension / neutral / compression (0.27 / 0.52 / 0.22). On continental upper plates by name (Sunda, Eurasia, Amur, N and S America, Caribbean; 126) they are 0.11 / 0.67 / 0.21. This is an approximation: two regions' signs (Luzon, Philippines) are inferred.

The finding stands on its own: under today's constant, 62% of the planet's continental overriding trench would extend and only 15% shortens. So the shortening branch has acted on a small share of the margins all along, consistent with `belt_lithosphere` 'shorten' reaching only ~9% of belt node-steps (journal 107). No single law gives Earth's 67% neutral. Earth's trenches mostly follow their upper plates; the planet's upper plates move with a broader spread about their trenches. That is a property of the force law, not of this rule. So the trench law becomes part of the sweep: `rollback_rule` 'fixed' (`rollback_mm_yr`) or 'lallemand' (r = `rollback_sb04_mm_yr` − `rollback_sb04_slope` × Vsub, Vsub the mean landward speed of the node's oceanic neighbours on other plates). It applies to both branches, because it is one identity. SB04 (Steinberger et al. 2004, hotspots moving in the mantle flow) is the frame tied to the deep mantle, as the model's world frame is the frame of its drag against the mantle. Rollback 9 is Lallemand's Table 2 SB04 mean.

**7. Defects and watch-points.**
- Only ~27% of the expected area is taken. Steps fall where the front node is already the plate's, does not touch its raster, is drawn twice, or the segment has no band (a margin narrower than 200 km). Read `backarc_area_m2` / `backarc_expected_m2` beside the thin share.
- No back-arc spreading. Crust stops thinning at 10 km, and a band thinned to the floor gives less volume, so its strip is thinner. A breakup into back-arc ocean (the Japan Sea, the Tyrrhenian) would be the next form.
- The rule acts on continental overriding plates only. Oceanic upper plates extending (Mariana-type) are booked (`overriding_ocean_extending_m`) but not modelled.
- The regime split is the planet's force law. No trench law gives Earth's 67% neutral margins. If the sweep adopts an arm, the upper plate's speed against its trench is the next question for the margins.
- Earth's Table 1 regime shares rest on regional signs (§2).
- `crust_budget`: the back-arc thinning and its strips are in no crust term. They cancel in the net, and the residual shows only their nearest-node jitter.
