**Reading.** A trench on the planet is born with a slab, accelerates its plate within a few Myr (slab pull, pole
turning toward it) and then lives as long as Earth's do. Earth's slow, slab-less oceanic convergence (17.4k km) never
gets that chance: forced convergence becomes self-sustaining subduction only after ~100–150 km of underthrusting
(Gurnis, Hall & Lavier 2004, G-cubed 5, Q07001; Hall et al. 2003, EPSL 212: ~100 km), and the cumulative convergence,
not its rate, sets the force balance. Items 3 and 4 read the population that today's rule leaves standing, so they
cannot measure a rule that stops slow births from becoming trenches; only a run can. An initiation rule is mechanism
with a measured value, and it gives the planet Earth's missing boundary class, whatever it does to T2.

**Built (default off): `subduction_initiation` 'convergence', `slab_init_km` 125** (the midpoint of Gurnis et al.'s
100–150 km; Hall et al.'s ~100 inside it).
- Each plate raster has a field `slab` (km of underthrust convergence; copied by split, merge, weld and hole fill;
  zero on new crust and off the mask).
- In `_classify`, after typing and polarity (`_slab_gate`): the overriding side of a subduction boundary (subduction
  nodes without the trench flag) reads the largest count of its band (`SLAB_BAND_RINGS` 2, kind N: the edge can move by
  up to two cells per step without losing it) and adds closing rate × dt. It writes the band back. A node has a slab
  once its count reaches 125 km, and the downgoing side within reach of such a node has one too. Every other subduction
  node becomes `CONVERGENT` (5): no trench flag, so no slab pull in `_driving`; no arc, subduction erosion or margin
  shortening (those read SUBDUCTION). There is no extra resistance (Hall et al. 2003: the resisting forces are small
  against the driving ones). Crust is still consumed by the overlap rule.
- A ridge-typed edge clears the band behind it (the slab detaches where the boundary opens), except where the band
  also lies behind a convergent edge. The count spreads along the edge by up to two reaches per step, which is how a
  slab extends as its boundary lengthens.
- A pair made by the subduction-initiation event starts with its slab: old ocean founders on its own (spontaneous
  initiation; Stern 2004, EPSL 226).
- The start has no slabs; its random-pole trenches gain them in 1–5 steps.
- `simulate_plates` stats add `convergent_no_slab_km` (node count); `erosion_totals` adds
  `convergent_no_slab_node_km_steps`.
- Tests (4 new, 56 in `test_plates`): `test_subduction_initiation_instant_is_the_default`,

**Reading.**
- The rule works as designed and in the right direction. Its slab-less convergence closes as slowly as Earth's OCB
  (p50 9–18 mm/yr). It takes 0.2–1.4k km off the intra-oceanic trench on the same states.
- But the planet makes only ~1k km of such convergence against Earth's 17.4k. Its new convergent boundaries start fast:
  the plates on both sides already move at 3–9 cm/yr, set by their other boundaries. They pass 125 km within a few Myr
  whether they pull or not. A gate cannot create slow convergence.
- T2 needs ~−14k km; this lever gives ~−0.7k. A six-seed sweep of pair + rule would be decided by pair polarity's
  chaotic swing (±3–9k km within 25 Myr here), which the Trench Flux sweep already measured.
- Neither change fragments plates within 25 Myr on the same state. The pre-check's runaway is the trajectory's own.

**Decision (Claude, by the goal; not a user decision): no sweep.** `Slab Initiation Sweep.cmd` was drafted but not
placed. `subduction_initiation` stays 'instant' and the code stays (default off). It is Earth's mechanism and should
return once the planet has slow convergence for it to act on. The polarity fix stays waiting, as in the forty-first
pass.

**What T2 needs, restated.** The planet lacks slow relative plate motion, not a gate:
- Its ocean–ocean convergence closes at p50 50–65 mm/yr (Earth's intra-oceanic SUB 60, OCB 7).
- Ridge production is 1.4× Earth's on Earth-like ridge length (opening p50 54 vs 34 mm/yr).
- Slab-less plates move at `v_min_cm_yr` 1.5 + the ridge term in whatever direction the normalized pull gives:
  `_driving` keeps only the pull's direction, so a plate whose ridge pushes cancel still moves at full target speed.
  Earth's slab-less plates (AF, EU, NA, AN, SA) move 1–3 cm/yr, and the boundaries between them are slow (OCB, CCB).
