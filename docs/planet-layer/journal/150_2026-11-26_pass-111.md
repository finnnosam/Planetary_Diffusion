By the letter the reading is R2 (first gap 4% of the final). But neither hypothesis fits: under C the mean age rises
5.5 Myr per Myr of run (1,209 → 2,582 in 250 Myr; the median reaches 3,600 Myr, the table's top, by 0 Myr), faster
than the clock, which no history of resets can do; under A it rises 1.3 per Myr. The rule did not foresee an age
running ahead of time; the reading added after seeing it is the clock bound, recorded as such.

Mechanism, checked by hand on `lithosphere.thickened_therm` (lith_frame 'lamb'): the Lamb table holds L at 114 km
from 270 to 770 Myr and at 178 km from 1,750 Myr (slope 1e-6 km/Myr), so a collision thickening of 0.01% turns age 300
into 770 and age 1,800 into 3,600. L barely moves at the jump, but the node then ages along the steeper stretch after
it. Both arms have this ratchet. Under 'overlap' the composition also reads the thermal age as a nine-point mean, and
the return writes the world value back onto the plate node wherever it changed, so ages mix in age space, where L is
concave: young lithosphere next to old takes a mixed age whose L is near the old one's. Which of the two carries C's
excess was not split.

Result and next arm: the low datum level under the remap is the lithosphere's (an age running ahead of the clock), not
the crust's. The next arm is a per-step trace of the continental thermal age on a warm state, both remaps, splitting
the change into aging, resets, the thickening jumps and the write-back, with its rule written first; behind it, the
two candidate fixes it would choose between (the thermal age read at the nearest plate node under 'overlap', as the
crust type and orogeny age already are; and a thickening rule that keeps the age on Lamb's flat stretches).
