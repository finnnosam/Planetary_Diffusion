**2. What the towers are** (`precheck-p65-towers/`: `tower_anatomy`, `tower_support`, `probe_towers`).
- At each run's highest snapshot, the top node split into Airy (its own crust + sediment on the Airy line, as
  `surface.elevation`'s `own_airy`), the dynamic topography and the rest, which is the elastic plate (Te 30 km)
  holding up a narrow load: control 4.1–6.2 km Airy + 1.6–6.7 km plate (the 11.2 km tower of seed 1004: 4.1 + 6.7);
  combo-02 3.7–5.5 + 3.1–4.9; no-flow arms 5.0–11.3 + 2.9–7.8. The no-flow peaks sit inside 50–500k km² above
  8.85 km of crystalline crust at 85–112 km (no sediment), with R on at Earth's foundering flux: the Airy part alone
  is 8–11 km. **The no-flow route is closed as a route to adoption:** with spreading at Earth's kernel and
  foundering at Earth's rate, crust without lateral flow piles to ~100 km; on Earth the lower crust of plateau-thick
  crust flows (Tibet, Altiplano). The flow's form and onset remain open (combo-06 below).
- The spikes' feeders, per step (`probe_towers.py`, today's PARAMS, cloud seeds 1007 and 1008, 250 Myr): the
  highest node's thickening this step by process (the flow, rift, subduction band and margin shortening, collision
  volume, erosion) and the jump when the world re-reads the plate rasters. Where the top node exceeds 7 km (40 and
  42 steps), the largest feeder there was the subduction term in 24 and 14 steps, the flow delivering in 4 and 17,
  collision 6 and 6, re-reads 6 and 5. Single steps put +85 km (seed 1007, −162 Myr) or +64 km (seed 1008, −116 Myr)
  on one node from the subduction term, with −76 / −46 km of erosion in the same step. Somewhere on the planet the
  subduction term exceeds 20 km in one Myr in 72 and 77 of ~250 steps, the collision dump in nearly every step
  (to 145 km). Earth's Andean thickening is ~2 km/Myr (shortening ~10 mm/yr of 40 km crust over a ~200 km belt).
- Cause of the subduction spikes (`crust_kernels.margin_shortening`): each trench segment (the nodes sharing a
  nearest overriding trench node) moves its foreland volume (350 ± 100 km behind the trench) onto its own belt nodes,
  renormalised to the belt weight the segment has. Where the belt is missing (an oceanic forearc in front of the
  continent, or only continental nodes in the far tail of the belt Gaussian) the whole foreland volume lands on those
  few nodes. A grid artifact, not Earth's process (a thrust wedge thickens the belt at the rate the trench feeds it).
  The collision dump is the one `collision_spread` 'earth' removes.
- First fix tried, along-strike pooling (`shortening_pool`: each segment's volume shared between the segments of the
  plate within σ = `foreland_sigma_km`, by belt weight; built and tested, cloud seed 1007 to −123 Myr): the term got
  worse (p50 of the per-step maximum 19 km against 14, worst 121 km) and led at all 13 steps above 7 km: the belt is
  missing along whole stretches of margin, not at single corners. Withdrawn before the sweep (code replaced).

**4. Built** (both off; default path identical step by step: n = 48, seeds 3 and 11, 70 steps each, threads 1,
state hashes against the unmodified tree; `tests/test_breakup_match.py` 6 passed).
- `shortening_match` (`crust_kernels.margin_shortening(..., match=)`): the belt is no longer renormalised to the
  nodes it has. Each belt node takes the trench flux at its own Gaussian weight, s × T_f × dt × g_belt (T_f the
  segment's g_fore-weighted foreland thickness), as the foreland gives s × T × dt × g_fore; the segment moves the
  smaller of the two volumes, both sides scaled to it (volume conserved). A node never thickens faster than the flux
  at the belt's peak: 2.7 km/Myr at s = 10 mm/yr on 40 km crust (Earth's Andes ~2 km/Myr). No new number.
- K, `split_rule` 'earth' (`events._split_events_earth`, `breakup.EARTH_RIFT_PRODUCTION`): every step, for every
  plate ≥ `split_min_area_mkm2` with continent, a candidate line (`_rift_line`, the existing rift line) and its
  continental margin L (both sides, the review's instrument); the split happens with probability
  1 − exp(−p(t) A_c dt / max(L, 200 km)), p(t) Earth's production per Mkm² by time since the start (the start mapped
  onto 250 Ma), A_c the plate's continental area. Expected margin per Myr is Earth's per unit continent while the
  rate per step is small (one split per plate per step at most: at the peak a plate with 60 Mkm² of continent and a
  2,000 km margin makes 0.47 of it, not 0.63). Splits that cross only ocean keep `split_rate_per_myr`; subduction
  initiation unchanged. `PlateSim.t_start` records the start.
