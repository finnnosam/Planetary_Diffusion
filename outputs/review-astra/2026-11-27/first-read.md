# Independent notes before opening the planning documents

I compared ETOPO, CRUST1.0, the dated ocean floor and a rasterized Earth plate map to the three distinct November 26 six-seed arms. I also reconstructed a no-surface-process endpoint from the archived seed-1001 fine-grid forcing. My numbers are in spatial.csv and replay.csv. Only code, data, run metadata and numerical outputs informed these notes.

1. The most damaging failure in the delivered land terrain is downstream. On the saved 30 Myr chain the plate parent has a 3,079 m land p99, the forcing with erosion and deposition switched off gives 5,553 m, and the current erodibility replay yields 1,557 m against Earth's 4,706 m. The *same places* that stand above 3 km on the parent average 4,448 m with forcing alone and just 638 m in the finished landscape. Missing mountain-building input alone cannot explain that result. Look first at time-dependent erodibility, denudation and the partition of forcing and flexure in those catchments. This is one matched seed.
2. Across the plate maps the excess is in middle elevations and missing coherent high country. The largest connected part of the highest 1% of land is 36% on the nearest arm and 27% on overlap with plate-area rifting, versus 95% on Earth. The near-normal thick-crust area and very low surface of the thickest columns warrant separating the distribution of thickness within the thick tail, its organization, and support at equal thickness. Earth's high tail is geographically exceptional.
3. Large interiors are interrupted by water. Land >500 km from any wet cell covers 21% (nearest) or 31% (overlap with plate-area rifting) against 45% on unglaciated Earth. Coast crossings are much too frequent and too steep. Separate scattered pits and islands from the connected open sea and large continents before attributing this to plate size or margin processes. Overlap improves these diagnostics but substantially reduces shallow-water area.
4. Ocean bathymetry has a plausible middle depth but deep adjacent cell differences are about half Earth's. The planet has more young seafloor and more plate-boundary crossings. Check age continuity and its relation to turnover separately from the small-scale relief of the abyss.

I would spend one diagnostic cycle on the landscape replay before another plate sweep. For the plates I would measure saltwater-connected interior distances and equal-thickness plateau support before choosing a mechanism. I see no present case for retuning the global datum or asking the meso to synthesize a missing plateau.
