# Planetary Diffusion | Independent terrain review

**22 November 2026 · n = 217 · Six 250 Myr worlds and the first 500 Myr world**

**The largest land failure is the lack of broad, low interiors.** Land below 200 m occupies 28.2% of generated land, close to Earth's 29.7%. Go 300 km inland and those shares become **3.8% and 19.2%**. The generator puts much of the right amount of low ground in the wrong place. Its maps have three times Earth's density of land–water crossings. Offshore, even deep floor of comparable age has less than half Earth's local relief.

**The planning loop has drifted in causal attribution and priority.** Earlier geography reviews identified the inland problem. Recent passes singled out the dynamic field on already-drowned crust. The field really does push many of those particular cells down. Remove the recorded field from all six saved worlds, conserve water and count the *whole population* again: the suspect depth band occupies **3.43% of the globe**, up from **3.27%**, against Earth's **0.25%**. Different cells take their place. Field placement matters, especially to the deepest tail, but this experiment undercuts the case for making its random component the next terrain intervention.

**I would keep the user's continental-level target and change the next experiment.** Trace the excess coast and missing inland plains to the operation that creates them. Correct the age-zero error at the landscape hand-off before interpreting fine-scale erosion. In the 500 Myr run, the continents lose volume and area while the surviving land rises. A longer clock alone will not produce a more Earth-like world.

## Ranked findings

### 1. Continents have too much shoreline and too little low ground behind it

**High confidence in the failure. Its generating process is still open.** Matching emerged area by shifting the diagnostic shoreline still leaves only 45.4% of generated land with four dry neighbour rings and 3.86% with a low, flat interior. Earth's equivalents are 68.8% and 14.24%. Measuring distance from only the largest connected water body raises the model's four-ring interior to 57.9%, while Earth's rises to 73.9%. Enclosed water explains part of the gap. The project's coast profile, rerun on the actual arm, independently gets **14.9% of generated land beyond 600 km from water versus 36.5% on Earth**. At 600–1,200 km the median height is 907 m versus 449 m. All six seeds have too few inland lowlands. [Sensitivity tests](../../outputs/review-astra/support.txt) · [Recovered coast profile](../../outputs/review-astra/coast-profile.txt)

The 46 km maps have **57.0 land–water crossings per million km²** on unique chart-axis edges, versus **19.1 on Earth**. This is a roughness indicator, not a physical coastline length. Eight-connected components give a mean of **382 one-cell islands and 377 one-cell water holes** per model globe versus 29 and 13 in the unmasked Earth region. The area excluded on Earth cannot explain that ratio. In the model, 97% of one-cell water-hole area is continental and surrounded by continental neighbours. Median hole-to-neighbour contrast, averaged over seeds, is **−261 m of elevation**, **+0.4 m of dynamic topography** and **−168 m of bulk sediment thickness**. The broad field can move a rough surface across sea level. It does not supply the local hole relief. These elevation holes are distinct from the plate-ownership pinholes in the test suite. [Component counts](../../outputs/review-astra/coast-components.csv) · [Local contrasts](../../outputs/review-astra/shore-source.csv)

Earlier work reached part of the same diagnosis. Journals 117 and 123 distinguished how much far-inland land exists from how low that land is. The drift is that these outcomes stopped determining what to investigate next. A smoother coast alone could improve the first while leaving the second poor. ### 2. The ocean is too smooth and renews too fast

**High confidence in both signatures. Moderate confidence in their connection.** Require the centre and all immediate neighbours to be dated floor, deeper than 1,500 m and within the same age band. On 20–60 Myr floor, median relief is **127 m generated versus 281 m Earth**. At 60–100 Myr it is **119 versus 301 m**. The regional four-ring deficit is also large. Yet the project's mean floor depth below −1.5 km on the datum is **4,203 versus 4,205 m**. This agreement establishes basin capacity much better than it establishes seafloor geography. [Support-matched measurements](../../outputs/review-astra/support_probe.py) · [Basin budget](../../outputs/planet-calibration/sweep-20261122-150810/reviews/crust_budget.txt)

The last-100-Myr contour review gives **95,980 km of ridge** against Bird's 94,800, but **5.19 km²/yr of production** against Bird's 3.37. The independent present-day age inventory gives 4.98 versus Seton's 2.99. Check opening velocity integrated along ridges before changing ridge lengths. There are **34.8 thousand km** of ocean–ocean convergence in the model window. Earth has **38.2 thousand** counting both subduction and other oceanic convergence. The model calls all 34.8 thousand trench; Earth divides into **20.8 thousand trench and 17.4 thousand other convergence**. Geological and kinematic classes differ, but this isolates a testable problem with instant initiation. The default speed law also uses unsigned boundary lengths with a separately relaxed direction. In the elevation law, fitting the conditional mean basement depth to Earth supplies no within-age relief. Blaming one process for both discrepancies would be premature. ### 4. The landscape hand-off gives active belts old-rock erodibility

**High confidence in the error and its input footprint. Its effect on finished elevation and incision is unmeasured.** The plate writer supplies `time − last_orogeny`, which equals zero in an active belt. `PlateHistory.sample` replaces missing values with zero before interpolation. `build_forcing` then sends every sampled age ≤0 to 10,000 Myr. A uniform active patch gets erosion multiplier **0.15**; the young-belt law gives **1.0**. Mixed missing/finite stencils also lose the distinction. The landscape uses this present-day erodibility map throughout its replay window. ### 5. Broad high plateaus are missing even though tower checks pass

**High confidence against present Earth. Moderate priority as a universal requirement.** The largest eight-connected area above 4 km averages **0.082 million km²** (largest of six 0.227), versus **2.10 million km²** on Earth. The >4 km land share stays between 0.15% and 0.19% in the six-world means at five epochs from −40 to 0 Myr. All six maximum-height integrity checks pass. ## Where the planning inference breaks

**Selection changes the causal conclusion.** “Drowned normal crust” is a narrow observable: crystalline thickness ≥30 km and height **−1,000 to −200 m** relative to sea. It excludes the shallowest and deepest drowned crust. On the frozen bodies I removed the recorded loaded field, re-solved each world's original water volume and reselected all eligible cells. **64% of original band area leaves; 67% of new band area consists of entrants.** Hence the modest net increase from 3.27% to 3.43% despite large movements on the selected mask. Among *all* normal crust, the share deeper than 1 km drops from 1.68% to 0.68%, while the share below sea rises from 19.91% to 22.82%. The low, flat interior share of all land rises from 3.59% to only 5.02% after removal, compared with 14.24% on Earth. Static removal retains the historical effects on erosion and sediment, the lithosphere, flexure and previous load decisions. A long-term field effect remains possible. [Manipulation](../../outputs/review-astra/attribution_probe.py) · [Population changes](../../outputs/review-astra/checks.txt)

As a selection check, I rotated each saved field through all 23 nonidentity proper cube rotations over its frozen body and conserved water. The six-world mean field *on the reselected band* is −533 to −462 m across rotations, compared with −421 m in the actual worlds. The rotations give **4.02–5.70%** drowned-band area, all higher than the actual 3.27%. A strong low on a subset defined by low elevation arises even with arbitrary placement. This rotates the already-loaded field, including swells, breaking historical and local loading consistency. It is a negative control on inference, not a replacement mantle simulation. ## Decisions and next work

The **user** set the realism rule, chose continental level on the datum with land fraction on 19 November, agreed to unpark field placement on 21 November, and prioritized continental geography over meso retraining. The user also set integrity-only vetoes and the build/sweep cadence on 4 November, delegated within-target ordering on 8 November, and asked for repeatable control borrowing and occasional longer runs on 21 November. The **planner** chose the decomposition, the drowned-normal subset, the clipped correlation share, and the order that puts another residual-field experiment ahead of wider geography and basin turnover. Authorizing field placement did not prescribe those choices. [4 November working rules](../planet-layer/journal/86_2026-11-04_pass-64.md) · [19 November target](../planet-layer/journal/122_2026-11-19_pass-93.md) · [21 November field decision](../planet-layer/journal/131_2026-11-21_pass-98.md) · [Current order](../planet-layer/STATE.md)

The build cadence addressed a real problem: eleven passes successively re-aimed without adoption. Journal 121 replaced a brittle final-step plate-ownership pinhole test with a persistence check. Journal 140 documented a numerical defect that invalidated its own earlier assumption about deterministic controls. Both are legitimate corrections. The later failure is the converse. In passes 100, 102 and 104 a plausible candidate could become the default by passing integrity tests even when its terrain benefit was uncertain. Predeclaring an adoption rule guards against moving the goalposts. It cannot establish that the candidate's premises are true. Keep integrity vetoes and allow the conclusion **“implemented correctly; geography benefit unresolved.”** If the cadence prevents finishing a causal comparison, I recommend that the user expressly allow an evidence-only checkpoint. That would change a user directive. This review changes no working rules.

1. **Follow the shoreline and lowlands through individual operations.** Before selecting on height, mark parcels by crystalline thickness, distance to a material continental edge and prior boundary history. Track lowland area, dry-interior area, shoreline crossings and entrants/exits in the depth band. Read them before and after plate-to-world composition and return transfer, erosion/deposition, flexure and the sea solve. Include a smooth, rigidly rotating continent with unchanged material loads. If roughness appears in that null, prioritize conservative material transfer. If it appears only after erosion, trace sediment delivery and fill/spill. If it appears only during inundation, examine water connectivity and load decisions. The model's near-zero dynamic contrast across one-cell holes gives this test a clear starting point. Test an active patch, an old patch, a missing patch and a mixed interpolation stencil.
